# 1 — Welcome, Gamemaster

You are not running a Jedi academy.

You are running the night before a religion is born—when the word *Je'daii* is still a taste on a monk’s tongue, when lightsabers are toys that do not exist, when the Force has a thousand names and no shared Code, and when a black pyramid has already closed its doors on a dozen homeworlds.

Your players start at **level 9**. Treat them as founders and first Masters-in-waiting: dangerous, unfinished as a culture, already capable of breaking a camp with a bad decision. The drama is not “will we survive the goblin?” The drama is “will we invent something worthy of the green world that called us?”

### Tone

Mythic survival. Founding politics. Wonder that bites. Tython as teacher and antagonist. Balance as physics, not a slogan.

### How a session should feel

Open in motion. End on a choice. Let the ship—and later the land—react when the table tips toward cruelty or absolutism. Prefer situations over scripts. Prep three locations and three faces; trust the rest.

### Cold open

You may read the player crawl from Campaign Guide **Appendix C** (*DAWN OF THE JE’DAII*), then cut straight to the Threshold Halls. Do not lecture galactic history at the table. Let the pyramid teach the era.

# 2 — A Time Before Jedi

There was a time before the Jedi. Before the Sith. Before the Force was divided, named, and turned into a weapon.

The galaxy was older then, and far less certain of itself. Kingdoms rose beneath unfamiliar suns. Clans fought over worlds that had no place on any chart. Across a thousand cultures, the Force was worshiped as a god, feared as a curse, studied as a mystery, or dismissed as the fancy of mystics.

There is no Holonet to ask. No Republic to petition. No reliable commercial hyperlane into the Deep Core that ordinary beings can trust. Distance is still a kind of god.

Then came the Call.

Not a voice—though some heard one. Not only a dream—though many woke with the same image burned into memory: a black pyramid beneath an alien sky, waiting on a green and distant world.

Across scattered worlds, ancient vessels stirred. Some descended without flame or signal. Others rose from stone and sand, shedding ages like sleep. No pilot emerged. No messenger explained. Yet each vessel had come for the same purpose: those who could feel the Force.

Eight gathering ships wake across the galaxy. A ninth—the greatest—already waits at Tython. Your party does not arrive on that ninth ship. Their pyramid is one of the eight, locked in Session 0 to a calling that will one day become a temple.

When the doors seal and home falls into the dark between stars, the ordinary histories of the galaxy continue without these pilgrims. What happens next is the founding of something that will outlive every kingdom that refused the Call.

# 3 — The Call & Your Table

Session 0 is not a rules lecture with snacks. It is the moment you and your players agree what kind of myth you are founding.

### Build legends, not padawans

Each hero is **level 9**, Force-capable (Campaign Guide Chapter 10 paths A–D), from an approved homeland planet—species free, not locked to iconic natives. Reflavor later-era Jedi/Sith jargon as local Force traditions. Credits and coin die the moment boarding begins: from then on, want something? Craft it, or favor an NPC who can.

### The Party Tho Yor calling

Before boon visions, the table must agree on **one** calling. That pick is which gathering Tho Yor they woke aboard—and which future Kesh that ship’s resting place becomes on Tython.

**Balance cannot be chosen.** Akar Kesh and the ninth Tho Yor are already on Tython.

| Calling | Future Kesh | Landing flavor |
|---------|-------------|----------------|
| Science | Anil Kesh | Chasm approaches |
| Art | Bodhi | Masara coast/plains |
| Healing | Mahara Kesh | Deep Ocean approaches |
| Knowledge | Kaleth | Council / library ground |
| Teaching | Padawan Kesh | Masara inland academy-seed |
| Body | Stav Kesh | Ice Giant Range / martial |
| Mind | Qigong Kesh | Silent Desert caverns |
| Skill | Vur Tepe | Volcanic forge |

Tell them: *That is the Tho Yor you woke aboard—and the temple-seed Tython will ask you to found.* Add that their calling will one day shape **one fold** of the Order’s Creed—do not dump the full Ninefold poem yet.

Record calling → Kesh → landing region on the Episode 1 sheet (Guide Ch 13).

### Boons

Assign visions from the Chapter 11 pool—name, look, and dream only. Hide mechanical effects until play earns the reveal. Leftovers become ship finds or rival claims.

### What players may know

Share Appendix C (crawl + primer), Chapter 10 planet/Force lists, calling **words**, and later their own destined Kesh name. Do not hand the full nine-temple end-state list or the full Ninefold Code at Session 0.

Then open aboard the pyramid—or in the final climb as doors seal—and begin Episode 1: **The Calling**.

# 4 — Aboard the Tho Yor

For several sessions, this ship is not a hallway between cutscenes. It is a small city under vow—and if you skimp on it, the landing will feel like a video-game level load instead of exile from Eden.

Eight gathering pyramids wake across the galaxy. Yours is the one locked in Session 0 to a calling. Sister ships hang as silhouettes in the galleries. The ninth—the greatest—already waits at Tython; your party does not sleep under that one. After arrival, your ship will rest at the seed matching their calling. Until then, these decks are the only sky they have.

> **Sidebar — Full Scene Cards**  
> Every room below has a Campaign Guide **Chapter 21** card (long GM Inspiration + short read-aloud + secrets). This chapter is continuous narrative for *you*. At the table, prefer the Ch 21 read-alouds.

## Living aboard

Treat routines as content. Days run **31.5 standard hours**; homeworld “days” already feel wrong—the ship is teaching Tython’s rhythm before anyone smells rain. Meal times argue. Someone invents a translation circle on the Threshold dais. Someone else claims a warm wall for craft and will trade favors for scrap. Children invent games that look like politics. Adults invent politics that look like games.

There is no market of coins. Credits are a joke told by people who have not understood the seal on the Call-door. Want a blade? Bring Torv bone and glass. Want a cloak? Bring Shekk-Arra a hide taken clean. Want a charm? Bring Miri something that once touched a moon—or pretend you will, and watch her eyes brighten too much.

The ship’s song is always underfoot: companionable in the dormitories until shared dreams make strangers co-conspirators; loud enough in the Cores to rearrange a vow; thin and watchful in the Vaults. Extreme violence can earn a **null-corridor**—sound dies, Force softens, offenders cool until they can share a deck without becoming predators in a cage. From the Galleries, other pyramids hang like rival novels mid-chapter. Contact waits for Tython; kinship and jealousy begin as geometry.

Murals and pilgrim crafts may quietly echo the party’s Session 0 calling—Science’s measuring marks, Art’s color, Body’s sparring scars—without dumping the nine-temple end-state. The Call-door will not reopen. Home is a story you tell in the dark to people who do not share your language yet.

Prep three Scene Cards a session. Let the party invent watch rotations and craft corners so that when the spines open, leaving hurts.

## Threshold Halls

The Threshold is not a corridor so much as a canyon carved by someone who measured in prayers. The stone is matte black shot with gold veins that do not quite follow stress lines—more like scar tissue that learned geometry. Light wells up from inside the walls; there are no sconces to steal, no seams for a knife. Gravity here is polite and slightly late: boots land a half-breath after the mind expects them to, which makes first-timers look drunk and veterans look careful.

Murals run the length of the hall—faces of species the party has never met, painted with a patience that feels like surveillance. The ship’s song is a low harmonic under the sternum, the kind of note that makes teeth itch and arguments quiet. Pilgrims cluster by language the way soldiers cluster by unit; someone is already inventing hand-signs; someone else cries without sound because the Call-door behind them will not reopen.

Ahead, three mouths of passage yawn open (dormitories, galleries, deeper ship). One door stands shut without a handle, as if the builders disliked knobs. The Force in this hall feels like a held breath—not hostile, not kind. It is waiting to see whether these strangers will become a people or a riot.

**Read-aloud (table):** *The corridor is wider than a canyon and quieter than a tomb. Soft light bleeds from the stone itself—no torches, no seams. Murals of faces you have never met watch you with patient eyes. Your footsteps land late, as if gravity is thinking. Ahead, three passages yawn open… and one door stands shut without a handle. Something behind it is listening for a reply.*

**Plant:** Three strangers meditating open the sealed door. A boon niche may glow (Guide Ch 11). Map 1 Crossroads (Guide Ch 23) is this social battlefield with sanctuary manners.

## Dormitories of the Called

The dormitory wing smells like travel packs opened too fast—old leather, dried fruit, sweat, and the sharp green of someone’s homeworld herb stuffed into a pouch for luck. Cells open off a spine corridor: true rooms with niches cut into stone, or alcoves with a lip of railing and a privacy weave hung by pilgrims who refuse to be naked before strangers.

Soft light dims and brightens on the long cycle that argues with every body. People dream loudly. Sometimes the dreams agree—same black pyramid, same green world—and strangers wake staring at each other like co-conspirators. Personal relics sit on shelves the ship somehow provides: prayer beads, clan knives, a child’s carved animal, a ledger with no currency left that matters.

Theft happens because fear happens. Bonding happens because the alternative is sleeping alone next to a wall that hums like a throat. If violence spikes, a side passage can go null. A faint niche may glow near a bedroll (*Milk of the Sword*). The playable tension: whose rules govern the night—homeworld custom, or the ship’s quiet insistence that strangers share breath?

**Read-aloud:** *Soft light breathes on a longer day than your bones expect. Sleeping niches line the walls; packs and relics claim shelves that should not exist. Someone dreams in a language you almost understand. A privacy weave flutters. From somewhere deeper, the ship’s song presses against your ribs—and a niche by an empty bedroll glows like a held secret.*

## Observation Galleries

The galleries are a wound of transparent stone looking into travel that refuses to be hyperspace. Outside, ribbons of Force-light braid around distant pyramid silhouettes—other Tho Yor, other cargoes of the Called—close enough to feel like neighbors and far enough to feel like myths. Stars smear in directions that make navigators swear and then go quiet, because swearing does not help.

Ahead, a green point thickens into a world; two companion lights attend it—one clean, one bruised—**still without names**. Do not let yourself or the players christen them Ashla or Bogan yet. The rails are warm where many hands have gripped them. A glyph cluster near a “helm” alcove rejects steering with the polite firmness of a parent taking a knife from a child: the panel cools, the song dips, the ship continues.

Someone will try to count the pyramids. Someone will bargain with gods that are not listening. Someone will laugh because if they do not laugh they will scream. Optional twin-pole mural on an inner wall supports later Core teaching without moon labels. Panic wants a pilot; the ship wants witnesses.

**Read-aloud:** *The gallery is a wound of glass looking into something that is not hyperspace. Ribbons of Force-light braid around distant pyramid silhouettes—other Tho Yor, other pilgrims. Stars smear wrong. Far ahead, a green point thickens into a world with two companion lights—one clean, one bruised—still without names. Someone gasps. The ship does not ask your permission to continue.*

## Meditation Cores

The Core is a hollow sphere large enough to make voices small. The ship’s song is loudest here—felt in teeth, in the hollow behind the eyes, in the places people keep private vows. Pilgrims enter barefoot if they are wise; boots feel like insults. The air tastes faintly of rain and copper.

When the teaching comes, it does not arrive as scripture on a wall. Two words settle into the mind as if they had always been waiting under the tongue: **Ashla** and **Bogan**—clarity that costs, hunger that costs—Force poles, **not moon names**. The chamber offers trials the way a blade offers an edge: mercy that spends strength, power that spends kindness. Refuse both and the floor may choose for you—a tremor that foreshadows Tython’s intolerance for imbalance.

An alcove may hold a shallow bowl that smells of first rain. Watch faces: zealots brighten; soldiers brace; children ask if the words are names of people. Your job is not to lecture theology. Your job is to let the ship teach, then ask what the PCs do with the scar.

**Read-aloud:** *The chamber is a hollow sphere. The ship’s song is loud enough to feel in your teeth. Two words arrive that no homeworld taught you—*Ashla. Bogan.*—clarity that costs, and hunger that costs. The Core offers a choice that is not a choice: mercy that costs strength, or power that costs kindness. Choose, or the floor will choose for you.*

## Machine-Spirit Interfaces

Crystal lattices tick like insect thoughts along walls that want to be understood by the wrong kind of mind. Glyphs rearrange when you look away—precise wrongness, unfriendly to ego. Science-minded pilgrims lean in like hunters; mystics flinch because the Force here is thin and sharp, like a tool left on a workbench. A panel warms under a curious hand; for a heartbeat the builders almost make sense—geometry as devotion—then the understanding bites and leaves a nosebleed or a week of bad dreams.

Deeper, a colder corridor smells of ozone and old rain. There the walls change: softer curves, older pride. Ring-within-ring. Two disks—one pale, one wounded. Many hands on one circle. Nine-fold refusal marks. The script is **not** this ship’s. PCs may sketch; they may not open a moon taxi yet (Guide Ch 08). Tension: open a wing for the council, or chase the colder truth that does not belong to the Tho Yor’s song.

**Read-aloud:** *Walls of crystal lattice tick like insect thoughts. Glyphs rearrange when you look away. A panel warms under a curious hand. For a heartbeat you almost understand the builders—then the understanding bites. Beyond the lattice, a colder corridor smells of ozone and old rain. There the walls change: softer curves, a ring inside a ring, two disks—one pale, one wounded—and many hands touching one circle. The script is not this ship’s.*

## Sealed Vaults

The vault wing is colder by design. Sound comes back wrong—short echoes, as if the air itself has been told not to gossip. One vault bears a nine-fold seal that feels less like a lock and more like a promise someone else made on your behalf. Pilgrims who linger too long report dreams of maps: nine resting places on a green world, or nine refusals, depending on how afraid they are.

A guardian presence may thicken here—not a politician, not yet a ghost with a speech, just pressure like being watched by a mountain. Adjacent, the ozone corridor toward the Kwa Gate antechamber continues the softer mural language. Do **not** fully open the vault in Arc I unless you are ready to hand the table a campaign treasure map. Tension: leave the seal and look like cowards, or pick at it and look like thieves before the Order even has a name.

**Read-aloud:** *The air drops ten degrees without a draft. A vault door waits under a nine-fold seal that does not care about your lockpicks. Beyond a colder arch, ozone and old rain—the same wrong script as the lattice corridor. Something large and patient seems to occupy the silence. The ship’s song is quieter here, as if it is holding its breath with you.*

## Confluence Amphitheater

The Amphitheater is the ship’s answer to a city square: terraces of stone descending toward a flat speaking floor, acoustics so fair that a soft voice carries and a shout embarrasses itself. Pilgrims fill the tiers by instinct—warriors mid-height for sightlines, healers near aisles, scholars too close to the floor. Translators sweat. Children climb railings until someone with a real stare sends them down.

A thin thread may be wound on a handrail (*Thread of the Uncut Hour*)—easy to miss if the table is busy inventing government. This is where strangers become a temporary people or a temporary war. Speakers-to-be test phrases. Someone will propose titles too early. Someone will demand blood for an insult that happened three decks away. The ship listens. Session 3 lives here—who speaks, who is guest, what Ashla and Bogan mean when half the room still says light and dark.

**Read-aloud:** *Stone terraces fall toward a speaking floor that makes whispers honest and shouts small. Hundreds of pilgrims pack the tiers—different skins, different weapons, same exhausted hope. Translators sweat between languages. A handrail catches a glint of strange thread. Someone calls for order in a tongue only a third of the room understands—and every eye turns toward whoever looks ready to answer.*

## Disembarkation Spines

The Spines are not stairs so much as the ship remembering how to become a bridge. When Tython receives them, ramps and spans of light-laced stone deploy with a sound like ice deciding to be a road. Wind from the Force Storm shoves at cloaks; rain may fall the wrong direction for a breath. Below: mud, ozone, trees blown outward from a flash that thought too hard. Above and behind: the pyramid that was home for weeks, suddenly a silhouette you might never sleep inside again.

Ordered landing means spines hold and the party leads toward the **Session 0 calling seed**. Scatter means a spine shears and the session becomes reunion math. Pilgrims freeze at the lip—not for fear of heights, for fear of meaning. Step first and become the story, or wait and become cargo that arrives late to its own myth. Akar approaches are for later pilgrimage—not default first camp.

**Read-aloud:** *The ship unfolds bridges of stone and pale light into the storm’s throat. Wind shoves. Below, a clearing waits like an open wound. Behind you, the pyramid’s shadow pools like spilled ink. The spine does not ask if you are ready. It only asks if you will walk.*

# 5 — Faces in the Song

Play the want, not the accent. One breath: who they are, what they want *right now*. Full toolkits and longer Inspiration: Campaign Guide **Chapter 22**.

### Senn-Vora — *Quiet Peak* (party ship default)

He moves like someone who spent centuries listening to stone that might never wake—and then it did. Dust-pale Talid robes; a voice that treats every word as if it might become law by accident. Not meek: paced. When pilgrims brawl, he arrives in the silence after the first punch and makes that silence useful. He wants an Order that listens. He fears the Call was vanity dressed as destiny. Use him early aboard for translation disputes and naming arguments. He may teach the word that becomes *Je'daii*—if the table earns it. Hook: stop a brawl without drawing blood.

### Crafters (credits are dead)

**Torv Maddan** — Burn-scarred forearms, honest hammer rhythm. Will not forge a purge. Meet him scavenging scrap aboard or building a forge from storm-wreck.  
**Shekk-Arra** — Armor that remembers the animal; hates wasteful trophies; Green Kin ally.  
**Miri Quill** — Sullustan focus carver; steadies Force use; Gate-moon materials make her talk too fast; no kill-purely hexes.  
**Vesh & Loom** — Two mouths, one workshop; need the PCs to choose a banner before there is an Order.

### Episode 1 walk-ons (drop one most sessions)

**Orrin Two-Songs** translates adult feuds with painful accuracy. **Silent Bran** offers trust in hand-signs. **Miller Cand** invents ration law with a knife for thieves. **Vault-Touched Ann** flinches at nine seals. **Echo-Priest Dal** hears the ship wrong—bleeding ears or holy ecstasy. **Pel the Map-Eater** tastes ink hunting chart edges. Promote favorites; the rest of the pool waits in Guide Ch 22.

### Sister-ship Speakers (silhouettes, then contact)

From the galleries you see other pyramids; on Tython you meet their voices:

- **Horruhn** — soft thunder for the living world  
- **Numa’Shar** — charm as armor; always a third option  
- **Iil & Vorr** — mercy with spine; tide-law healers  
- **Sari Tor** — blade honesty; challenges cowards in charge  
- **Kael Rind** — tired fortifier who fears becoming his old captain  
- **Yen-Ti** — curiosity as addiction; Gate and Chasm hunger  
- **Sister Luma** — kind knife; Ashla-only purity  

**Reth Var** crowns “safety through command.” **Kresh the Red** makes strength a religion. Stop them from becoming the Order’s first heresies—or let the table fail interestingly.

### Guardian Echo

Not a politician. A harmonic near the ninth Tho Yor’s pinnacle; a hooded silhouette without a face. Nod at Naming. Warn when the Gate is abused. Never monologue.

# 6 — The Green World

## Force Storm

Arrival is not weather; it is the Force having an opinion. Lightning forks toward fear, cruelty, loud imbalance. Wind carries half-heard names—some of them the PCs’. The ninth Tho Yor hangs above a stone pinnacle like a judge without a gavel. Sister pyramids stutter in cloud. Calm them and the Storm Clock eases; rule them by terror and it advances. Bodies wet, burned, euphoric, catatonic. This is the last uncomplicated hour of *home*.

**Read-aloud:** *Lightning thinks. Wind carries names you have not spoken aloud. The ninth Tho Yor hangs above a stone pinnacle like a verdict. Your ship shivers. Pilgrims scream in a dozen languages that somehow mean the same prayer: hold.*

## Storm-Scar Clearing

First real ground: mud that wants boots, a crater that smells like lightning with opinions, trees blown outward like a diagram of panic. The pyramid’s shadow is a second night. Predators taste Force-light and pilgrim fear equally. Claim the ring fast. Default first camp near the **Session 0 calling seed**—not Akar (Guide Ch 16). Map 2: Guide Ch 23.

**Read-aloud:** *Rain still falls upward for a breath, then remembers gravity. The clearing is a wound: mud, ozone, and a ring of trees blown outward from a single impossible flash. Your Tho Yor’s shadow pools like spilled ink. Something large sniffs the blood-warm ground. Behind you, pilgrims wait for someone to say here.*

## First night / Omen of the moons

Cookfires stitch a trembling civilization. Watch rotations argue while predators test the dark. Two companion moons hang—one clean, one hungry. **Before** the Omen beat they are only lights. **After** Speakers lock Ashla/Bogan for Force-talk, the camp names the moons after the concepts (Guide Ch 02 / 17). Until step two: twin lights, bright moon, hungry moon—nothing more. Crafters want materials. Children ask if the pyramid will leave. Adults pretend they have an answer.

**Read-aloud (before naming):** *Cookfires stitch a trembling ring against the dark. Two moons hang—one clean, one hungry—still waiting for words. Somewhere beyond the watch line, the forest answers a dream with footsteps. A child asks if the pyramid will leave them. No one has an honest answer yet.*

## Continents as sensory tours

**Thyr** — Silent Desert; sound dies; Qigong caverns under a floating pyramid; footsteps arrive late.  
**Masara** — Soft lies; Edge Forest; Bodhi beauty and Padawan clearings that want children taught.  
**Kato Zakar** — Weather with a grudge; Stav’s Tho Yor embedded in ice.  
**Chasm** — Equations and madness in one breath; Anil’s temptation.  
**Deep Ocean** — Heal and drown by the Force’s mood; Mahara’s tide-law.  
**Volcanic Forge** — Metal dreams; Vur Tepe judges makers by cooling ash.  
**Akar approaches** — Pinnacle under the ninth ship; pilgrimage gravity; not first camp.

Faction camps crystallize later as Listening Circle, Spear Lodge, Open Hand, Chasm Inquiry, Green Kin—places people gather before they are temples (Guide Ch 06).

> **Sidebar — Kwa Gate**  
> Moon road only: **Ashla** or **Bogan**. Three species touching the ring while calm. Sketch Arc I murals for payoff. Illegal destinations refuse. Guide **Chapter 08** + Map 4; surface Scene Cards in Ch 21.

# 7 — The Path Ahead

### Arc I — Aboard (Sessions 1–3)

Bond pilgrims. Tour Threshold, dormitories, galleries. Teach Ashla/Bogan as **concepts**. Glimpse Vault and Kwa murals. First council in Confluence. End on approach and storm light. Episode 1 checklist: Guide Ch 13.

### Arc II — Arrival (Sessions 4–6)

Storm. Disembark (ordered or scatter) toward calling seed. Survive. Claim ground. Bridge to Omen night.

### Arc III — Survive & Contact (Sessions 7–11)

Sister Speakers. Trade and mistrust. Predators and politics. Optional early Gate trial. Name the moons when both hang clear.

### Arc IV — Balance & the Name (Sessions 12–16)

Name the Je'daii. Ratify the **Ninefold Code**—party rewrites only their calling’s fold. Calendar workshop or Twin Measure. First trial for imbalance. Level 12 milestone.

### Arc V — Temples of the Dawn (Sessions 17–24)

Seed 3–5 temples in play; montage the rest. Travel hardships become the Great Journey myth. Cornerstone rites; stewards left behind.

### Arc VI — Capstone (Sessions 25–30)

First Council. Codify the Journey. Resolve zealot, purge, or Chasm breach. Epilogue: children recite the Creed; nine lights on Tython.

Milestones and session estimates: Guide **Chapter 14**.

# 8 — Temples of the Dawn

These are not temples yet. They are resting places waiting for cornerstone and myth. Full Scene Cards: Guide Ch 21. Party home seed matches Session 0 calling; Akar is the ninth ship’s claim.

**Akar** — Balance pinnacle; already waiting; sacred gravity.  
**Anil** — Chasm science; lean too far and pay in mind.  
**Bodhi** — Beauty that demands kinship.  
**Mahara** — Flame cupped against wind; compassion made visible.  
**Kaleth** — Memory and council; who forbids what.  
**Padawan** — First discipline; body as first temple.  
**Stav** — Martial vow in ice.  
**Qigong** — Silence until distance speaks as near.

# 9 — The Ninefold Creed

The Order’s shared Code is not a three-line Jedi echo. It is the **Ninefold Code**—one stanza per temple—printed as defaults in Campaign Guide **Chapter 07**.

At the Naming Council, present the Creed. Confirm which fold matches Session 0 calling. The table **rewrites only that stanza**. The other eight stay—including Akar, which they cannot own via calling. Sister Speakers are assumed to have brought the rest, or the council simply adopts the printed text.

Millennia later, after Force Wars far outside this campaign’s scope, Jedi and Sith will each keep a twisted inheritance of this Creed. That schism is lore flavor only. Your table’s job is to write the original—one fold at a time.

# 10 — Running the Table

**Scene Cards.** Skim GM Inspiration privately. Speak the short read-aloud. End on the hook. Never script the solution. (Guide Ch 21.)

**Storm Clock.** Imbalance advances weather and pressure. Calm and Balance ease it. (Guide Ch 10.)

**Craft or favor.** No purchases with currency. Make it, or owe someone. (Guide Ch 10 / 13.)

**Spoilers.** Crawl and calling words are player-safe. Full temple list and Ninefold poem wait until earned—except the party’s own Kesh once calling locks. After Arc IV ratification, the Creed is shareable.

**If you freeze.** Ask what the nearest NPC wants next.

**Prep sheet.** Strong start. Three locations. Three faces. Eight portable secrets. One boon beat. Storm Clock note.

You have two books now: one to *run*, one to *feel*. Open the pyramid. Let the green world thicken ahead. And when the doors seal, remember—you are not joining the Jedi. You are inventing the people who will make that word necessary.
