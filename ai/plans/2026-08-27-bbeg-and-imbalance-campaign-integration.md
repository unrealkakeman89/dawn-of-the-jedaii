## 1. Executive summary

This plan replaces the earlier same-day version with a complete operational design record for integrating a central cult antagonist, a mandatory campaign-wide imbalance system, early required `Kwa Gate` discovery, and a reciprocal `Ashla`/`Bogan` restoration practice into future campaign implementation work.

The approved direction is not a named villain build. It is a planning posture: a Force-sensitive pilgrim arrived aboard a `Tho Yor`, quietly gathered followers, departed the main migration after landing, and is using a cult plus a still-undecided Dark Side site on Tython to push the planet toward darkness. The party must witness that imbalance directly, the `Imbalance Storm Clock` must become mandatory, and `Force Storms` must become required adventure pressure rather than optional flavor.

The major same-day expansion recorded here is that `Kwa Gate` existence and early discovery are now required planning facts, while activation, travel, reliable use, and network understanding still develop progressively. This plan also records the approved reciprocal restoration direction: dark-imbalanced people may be sent to `Bogan` to contemplate `Ashla`, while light-imbalanced people may be sent to `Ashla` to contemplate `Bogan`, with the goal of Balance and eventual return rather than permanent punishment. None of this authorizes implementation.

## 6. BBEG ideology

The ideological core already approved for planning is that Balance is weakness, coexistence is failure, and peace requires one victorious will strong enough to silence opposition. Tython's instability becomes the cult's proof-text: the world seems to react whenever contradiction, fear, hunger, and competing doctrine intensify.

| Ideological element | Status | Notes |

| Tython's instability as evidence | `APPROVED` | Storms and imbalance become recruitment material. |

## 7. Cult structure

| Layer | Who may fit | Function | Planning note |
|------|-------------|----------|---------------|

## 8. Recruitment and control model

| Phase | Recruitment posture | Control posture | Approved levers |
|------|----------------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Aboard the `Tho Yor` | Quiet listening, clique formation, selective sympathy, secrecy | Social isolation, selective access, promise of certainty | belonging, fear, resentment |
| Landing chaos | Reframe decisive command as mercy during `Force Storm` pressure | Move followers away from public accountability | protection, urgency, destiny |
| Early camp separation | Offer food, shelter, and identity to the frightened or humiliated | Prevent exit through shame, threat, or witness control | certainty, protection, power |
| Pool consolidation on Tython | Tie doctrine to a dangerous place, omen, or ritual proof | Harder obedience, harsher retaliation, deeper secrecy | ambition, fear, revelation |
| Wider expansion | Use storms, faction conflict, and public scars to recruit | Weaponize narrative, martyrdom, and selective restoration failure | certainty, power, retaliation |

Control is not limited to overt violence. It may also include ration dependency, secret-keeping, false protection, curated testimony, and doctrinal framing of every crisis.

## 14. First Migration development of the practice (12-step developmental sequence)

This practice must emerge through play rather than appearing as a fully mature institution on day one.

| Step | Development | Why it matters |
|------|-------------|----------------|
| 1 | The `Tho Yor` teaches `Ashla` and `Bogan` as Force concepts aboard ship | The vocabulary exists before the institutions do |
| 2 | `Kwa Gate` foreshadow and glimpse become memorable | The moons are thinkable as destinations before they are legal procedure |
| 3 | Arrival storms teach that imbalance has physical cost | Restoration will be about survival, not abstraction |
| 4 | The cult's departure and early harms create a problem ordinary camp justice cannot easily solve | The question becomes practical |
| 5 | Early physical Gate discovery makes moon travel concrete | Restoration can be imagined as action rather than metaphor |

## 30. Redemption and return

This campaign happens before later Jedi orthodoxy. Return from imbalance should be framed through Balance, witness, restraint, restitution, protection, and truth-telling, not as a single speech check.

| State | Meaning | Return test |
|------|---------|-------------|
| Surrendered | Immediate harm stopped | Not yet trusted |
| Under watch | Safety requires supervision | Must continue truth and restraint |
| Cooperating | Gives usable truthful help | Still may be self-protective |
| Protected leaver | Has turned away enough to need safety from the cult | Requires community burden |
| Under restoration | Participating in a visible process | Must survive more than one scene |
| Returned under condition | Back in camp with limits, witnesses, and duty | Trust remains partial |
| Reconciled | Some harmed people can accept guarded reintegration | Never means history disappeared |
| Relapsed | Returned to harm, domination, or deception | Must stay visible in the record |

No person should become "redeemed" because the table liked the speech.
