### Encounter Scaling (short)

Arc III assumes variable party size. When a beat turns dangerous, scale by:

- Number of player characters present in the scene
- Level 10-11 capability and access to Force solutions
- Whether allied NPCs are helping, panicking, or complicating the scene
- Whether the scene is mainly social, exploration, or tactical combat

For this candidate, the preferred scaling tools are additional bodies, better positioning, tighter timelines, more exposed civilians, and more expensive consequences. Use existing SW5e creatures, humanoids, or hazards if the table drops into combat instead of inventing unsupported numbers here.

## Central Reference Summaries (Arc III local)

### Exploration loop (full procedure)

Run Arc III outward from the camp and back into it. Every substantial outing should leave a social mark by the next cookfire.

1. Open with a named need or rumor from camp: food, water, a missing scout, wrong smoke, a tool problem, a route question, a ritual site, or fear that another camp is already closer than expected.
2. Send the party out with one practical question and one unsettling unknown. Companion Arc III guidance is explicit here: do not let exploration become a montage. The outing needs a place, a choice, and a cost.
3. Choose one primary discovery and, if time allows, one secondary marker. A discovery should give useful information and force a judgment about how much to take, whether to share it, and who gets to interpret it.
4. Make the return matter the same session. The loop is incomplete until the party brings back a consequence at camp.
5. Update the sketch map before scene end. A rough mark on leather, ash, bark, or Foundry notes is enough.
6. Promote one NPC consequence carrier whenever possible. Miller Cand, Horruhn, Silent Bran, Pel the Map-Eater, Senn-Vora, or a newly introduced scout should be the face of "what comes home."

| Discovery class | What to show in the wild | What should come home with it |
|-----------------|--------------------------|-------------------------------|
| **Food valley** | Relief that looks too easy: edible growth, clean-looking water, game trails, or a sheltered basin | Ration politics, ownership disputes, or the companion's shared-dream pressure if the valley is taken greedily or without respect |
| **Poison patch** | Abundance beside warning signs: wrong-scent flowers, stained runoff, numb leaves, or prey bones where good forage should be | Fear, blame, careful sampling, or a new camp rule about who gets to test safety first |
| **Rival smoke** | Another camp's fire, markers, songs, hunting pattern, or claim-signs on ridges and paths | A border problem, mistrust, a follow-home witness, or the first scheduled contact scene |
| **Temple-seed landmark** | Chasm breath, desert mouth, coast light, embedded ice, deep tide, volcanic ash, or another place that "wants" more than a campfire | Wonder, expedition pressure, faction claims, and map interest; do **not** reveal every Kesh name just because the land feels important |
| **Ruined pre-pilgrim traces** | Optional ancient stone, impossible carving, non-pilgrim route, or builder-adjacent mystery | Yen-Ti pressure, Pel's obsession, or a delayed mystery marker rather than immediate payoff |

If the party bypasses a discovery, note what remains unknown and let that absence matter later. Ignored valleys still feed someone. Unchecked smoke still belongs to somebody.

### Storm Clock (brief)

Use the Chapter 10 optional Imbalance Storm Clock if your table likes a visible pressure gauge.

- Advance the 6-segment clock when the camp or party commits a notably imbalanced act.
- At 3 segments, surface omens and subtle environmental tells.
- At 6 segments, trigger a Force weather event and reset the clock to 2.
- Clear 1-2 segments when the party acts in deliberate Balance: mercy and strength together, genuine mediation, refusing cruelty without becoming passive, or completing a shared rite without domination.

Arc III-specific triggers that fit the source material:

- Hoarding or lying about food or water
- Fear-based command decisions
- Purity rhetoric turning into punishment
- Dominance killings or attempted dominance killings
- Gate abuse, especially using moon travel as a purity weapon
- Sacrificing a weaker camp or noncombatants because it is "efficient"

Arc III-specific tells that fit the source material:

- Cookfires spit sideways or go quiet at the wrong moment
- Predators test the watch line earlier than they should
- Wrong wood smoke bites the nose before anyone sees the source
- Named arguments seem to change the weather after the Omen
- The twin lights feel closer than their actual distance

### Faction crystallization (brief)

Arc III begins with camp tendencies. It should end with recognizable coalitions, spokespeople, and preferred answers.

| Coalition | What they want | Early visible signs | Typical pressure on the PCs |
|-----------|----------------|---------------------|-----------------------------|
| **Listening Circle** | Monasteries, shared meditation, slow decisions | Tea, mats, quiet ring near Senn-Vora's people, arguments deferred until breathing settles | Support for Code-writing and restraint; resistance to militarized shortcuts |
| **Spear Lodge** | Walls, drills, chain of command, fast answers | Spear racks, training space, watch pride, Reth Var speeches, public readiness | Pressure toward raids, duels, hard borders, and decisive force |
| **Open Hand Healers** | Mahara access, laws against Force cruelty, survivable mercy | Salt-smell tents, ledgers of the saved and refused, Iil/Vorr-style rules talk | Demand that harsh solutions justify themselves in public |
| **Chasm Inquiry** | Access to Tho Yor interfaces, theories, forbidden understanding | Chalk, crystal splinters, diagrams, curious volunteers, Yen-Ti bargains | Offer breakthroughs and shortcuts with long tails |
| **Green Kin** | Protect living places from overbuilding and careless extraction | Moss markers, grove-edge camps, wrong-wood alarms, Horruhn's quiet jurisdiction | Force the table to ask whether survival is becoming conquest |

Antagonist pressures remain active even when no single villain is present:

- Pure Ashla zealots treat peace as purity enforcement
- Pure Bogan zealots treat strength as moral proof
- Warlord pilgrims treat fear as civic glue
- Tython itself punishes imbalance

## Session 7: Scout Loop and First Discoveries

**Arc beat:** The camp stops being a landing site and becomes a scouting base.

**Strong start:** Open with a named need from Miller Cand, Silent Bran, Horruhn, Pel the Map-Eater, or another camp worker: food, water, spoor, smoke, pathfinding, or proof that a rumored landmark is real.

**Must-play beats:** Run one complete scout -> discovery -> camp consequence loop. Put at least one near-wild feature and one social marker on the table map before session end.

**Map use:** Theater default. A shared sketch map, journal page, or player handout blank should already be visible.

### Scene - Scout Valley

| Field | Content |
|-------|---------|
| **Scene Purpose** | Teach the Arc III exploration loop with a first useful discovery that is practical, unsettling, and not yet overexplained. |
| **Trigger** | Camp needs immediate information about food, water, safe route, predators, materials, or whether a nearby landmark is worth the risk. |
| **Immediate Situation** | The party leaves the storm-scar camp and reaches a near valley or low basin that looks more generous than the landing zone: water, edible growth, spoor, or unusual stone draws them in. Relief is visible. So is the possibility that relief has terms. |
| **Read-Aloud** | The valley opens below the camp ridge in a spill of wet green and dark stone. Water moves somewhere under the brush. Fruit, fungus, or grazing sign crowds the low ground like an answer that arrived too quickly. The air is cleaner here than the storm-scar camp, but not calmer. Every useful thing stands close to something that wants to be noticed. |
| **GM Notes** | Choose one primary discovery and one pressure point. Good source-backed options: a food valley that turns strange if stripped too hard; a poison patch that punishes greed but can be sampled carefully; a landmark that clearly matters but should not be named as a future Kesh yet; optional pre-pilgrim traces for later mystery. End on a decision: take, mark, share, or push deeper. |
| **Scene Card summary** | Tone: hopeful risk. Senses: wet growth, old ozone, shifting light, animal quiet that may mean safety or warning. Secret pressures: abundance is not free; wonder creates faction claims; the first map line is really a first political choice. |
| **Location** | Near-wild valley, grove, shore pocket, ridge basin, or region-appropriate equivalent within a hard walk of camp. Flavor it by the party's calling seed region without prematurely naming every temple destiny. |
| **Present Characters** | Player characters plus one or two scout companions such as Silent Bran, Tiny Ro, Pel the Map-Eater, a hungry forager, or a cautious watch-keeper. Keep the number flexible for party size. |
| **Objectives** | Secure practical value, mark the route, learn whether the place can be revisited safely, and decide what story about it reaches camp first. |
| **Opposition** | Getting lost, overharvesting, misreading warning signs, frightened scouts, predators following scent or Force-light, and the temptation to declare a place "ours" too quickly. |
| **Mechanics** | Call for checks only when method matters. Good fits include Wisdom (Survival) to keep the route, Intelligence (Investigation) to separate blessing from bait, Wisdom (Perception) to catch spoor or taint, Dexterity (Stealth) to move without spooking the site, or Strength (Athletics) if the route turns physical. No fixed DCs, damage values, or fabricated exhaustion costs are set here. On a bad outcome, the discovery should still teach something and come home as trouble instead of vanishing. |
| **Clues** | Tython can feed and punish in the same breath. Not every useful place is safe to strip. A nearby landmark may matter more than food and still remain unnamed. Other camps are not the only forces leaving claim-signs in the region. |
| **RP Guidance** | Ask who decides what "enough" means. Reward careful notes, scent marks, tied reeds, stone stacks, and other table-invented fieldcraft. Let practical and mystical interpretations argue without making one automatically correct. |
| **Developments** | The party returns with food, warnings, poison markers, a map line, a landmark sketch, an obsessed scout, or a rumor that now has evidence. |
| **Failure/Partial Success** | Partial success examples: food comes home but so does a shared dream; poison is correctly identified only after someone carried it; the route is found but a predator learns the camp; the landmark is mapped but the party returns too late to control the camp's reaction. |
| **Alternate Approaches** | Harvest and leave; take samples only; shadow animal trails; use Force meditation to read the land; split roles between fast scouts and slow mappers; retreat as soon as the valley proves it has a price. |
| **Transition** | Move to `Rival Smoke Ridge` if another camp's sign is visible from higher ground, or cut straight to `Camp Return Consequence` if the valley itself already created the problem. |
| **Consequences** | The first real map mark appears. Camp now has something to hope for, fear, hoard, or argue over. The Storm Clock may move if the party treats the discovery as entitlement instead of stewardship. |
| **Continuity Updates** | Record what was found, what was left behind, who argued for which choice, and how the discovery was described when the party got home. |
| **Foundry Assets** | Theater default. A sketch map handout or note page is usually enough. |
| **Source Traceability** | CW-030; Chapter 17 `Exploration loop`; companion Chapter 7 `The exploration loop`; Chapter 05 region notes; candidate connective only for return-state bookkeeping. |

### Scene - Rival Smoke Ridge

| Field | Content |
|-------|---------|
| **Scene Purpose** | Turn "other camps exist" from rumor into a visible fact and set up Session 8 contact pressure without forcing a full trade scene early. |
| **Trigger** | The party climbs for bearings, follows spoor, smells wrong wood smoke, finds claim-signs, or chooses to verify whether the region is already being watched. |
| **Immediate Situation** | From a ridge, tree line, or stone shelf the party spots another fire-ring or route marker. Smoke rises where no one from their camp should be. The approach is already marked by cloth, moss, shell cord, stacked stones, or another culture-specific sign. |
| **Read-Aloud** | From the ridge the land unfolds in layers of dark green and pale stone. Smoke threads up from a second fire-ring where no one in your camp should be. Strips of cloth, moss knots, or shell cords mark the approach before you see faces. Someone else already decided this ground matters. |
| **GM Notes** | Choose the visible claim style from source-backed coalition language: Spear Lodge flags, Green Kin markers, tide-law strings, or another Speaker's practical sign system. This scene can stay at observation range or become a brief tense hello, but it should not spend all of Session 8's diplomacy budget in advance unless the table forces it. |
| **Scene Card summary** | Tone: wary reconnaissance. Senses: smoke on wet wind, distant voices, claim-signs in paths, watchful silence. Secret pressures: signs can be read correctly or insultingly; a retreat still leaves an impression. |
| **Location** | Ridge, high root line, coastal rise, broken escarpment, or other overlook that can see camp smoke and return routes. |
| **Present Characters** | Player characters, scout companions, and one or more distant watchers or envoys from another camp. The exact sister camp can stay provisional until the party commits to contact. |
| **Objectives** | Identify who is nearby, decide whether to signal, avoid accidental insult, and come home with a real social mark for the map. |
| **Opposition** | Misreading signs, looking like raiders, being seen taking notes, crossing a boundary without permission, or assuming every distant silhouette is hostile. |
| **Mechanics** | Use rolls only when uncertainty matters. Wisdom (Insight) can read posture, Dexterity (Stealth) can keep the party unseen, Wisdom (Perception) can catch watcher placement, Intelligence (Investigation) can study claim-signs, and Charisma (Persuasion) can support a deliberate peace signal. If combat breaks out, make it a warning clash, retreat pressure, or captured-pride moment instead of the act's only answer. |
| **Clues** | Another camp is close enough to contest routes and supplies. Its signs already reveal priorities. The first border problem can be scheduled before either side draws blood. |
| **RP Guidance** | Ask the players what a peace sign looks like from their culture before you ask whether it works. A visible child, wounded hunter, or supply burden can complicate easy enemy framing. |
| **Developments** | The party gains a border landmark, a warning, a token, a scheduled meet, a mistaken insult, or a watcher who quietly follows them home. |
| **Failure/Partial Success** | Watchers may vanish and force camp to interpret the sign secondhand. A too-bold scout may start Session 8 with the other camp already expecting disrespect. A peaceful retreat can still produce mistrust if it looks like spying. |
| **Alternate Approaches** | Stay hidden and map only; leave a visible token; hail openly; send one envoy; withdraw and let Senn-Vora or another anchor frame the next meeting. |
| **Transition** | Return to camp with a token, warning, or mistrust problem. If a watcher follows, reveal that fact during `Camp Return Consequence`. |
| **Consequences** | A second camp enters campaign reality. The map now has a social edge, not only terrain. Session 8 starts with genuine contact pressure instead of abstract intention. |
| **Continuity Updates** | Mark smoke, markers, and the party's chosen posture on the map. Note whether the other camp now sees the party as cautious, honorable, nosy, or predatory. |
| **Foundry Assets** | Theater default; ridge sketch or camp marker overlay if desired. |
| **Source Traceability** | CW-030; Chapter 17 `Exploration loop`; companion Chapter 7 `rival camp smoke`; Chapter 06 coalition signs and pressures; Chapter 22 walk-on pool as needed. |

### Scene - Camp Return Consequence

| Field | Content |
|-------|---------|
| **Scene Purpose** | Complete the exploration loop by proving that every useful discovery returns to the cookfires as a practical or political problem. |
| **Trigger** | The scouts come home with food, signs, poison, a token, a map mark, or a silent worry they cannot bury. |
| **Immediate Situation** | The camp has already started reacting before the full report lands. People are hungry, wet, proud, tired, and ready to overread what the scouts brought back. A useful thing or dangerous rumor is now in public view. |
| **Read-Aloud** | Cookfires throw a thin ring of light around the camp. Steam lifts off wet cloaks. Half-built shelters lean into the dark like tired shoulders. Before you finish setting your packs down, people are already looking past you toward what you carried home. |
| **GM Notes** | Pick a consequence that fits the discovery. Strong source-backed choices: Miller Cand opens a ration argument; a stolen tool rumor becomes immediate diplomacy fuel; a predator followed the scouts' scent or Force-light; Horruhn smells wrong wood smoke; Pel the Map-Eater tries to turn wonder into obsession. Senn-Vora is a useful **INDEPENDENT_OF_CALLING** camp anchor here, but do not let him solve the scene for the party. |
| **Scene Card summary** | Tone: fragile civilization under pressure. Senses: smoke, wet cloth, bad sleep, too many witnesses, just enough light to turn every rumor into a shape. Secret pressures: useful news becomes policy fast; the camp is already sorting who gets heard first. |
| **Location** | Main cookfire ring, ration stores, watch line, healer tent edge, or another camp-common area where many people can react at once. |
| **Present Characters** | Miller Cand, Senn-Vora, one or two scene-appropriate scout or crafter NPCs, the returning party, and whichever camp voices are necessary to turn the discovery into a real consequence. |
| **Objectives** | Deliver the report, keep the camp from making the problem worse, decide what becomes public knowledge, and lock the next session's contact pressure. |
| **Opposition** | Hunger, pride, fear, rumor, faction opportunism, and anyone who benefits from turning ambiguity into accusation. |
| **Mechanics** | Handle as a consequence scene first and a skill scene second. ## Player-Facing Handouts (Pilot)

These are pilot-facing aids for this candidate only. They do not replace source manuscripts or production handouts.

### Near-wild sketch map blank

Use a simple player-facing sheet with:

- Camp ring or Tho Yor shadow at the center
- Empty spokes or blank space for routes outward
- Space for labels such as `food`, `water`, `smoke`, `danger`, `quiet ground`, `witnessed trade`, `sacred?`, and `unknown`
- A separate margin for `ally`, `rival`, and `owed` notes
- No forced temple names unless the table has actually earned them in play

Minimal player instruction:

> Mark where you have been. Circle what fed you. Cross what lied. Leave blanks where you only have rumor.

### Contact vocabulary tips (pilot)

Keep this player-safe and practical.

- Say who you are before saying what you want.
- Open hands and visible gifts do more work than long speeches.
- Repeat back what you think the other camp meant before you answer it.
- Ask for witness, water, work, shelter, or trade plainly.
- Do not assume a taken tool means the same thing in every camp.
- Coins do not solve anything here. Food, labor, craft, escort, witness, healing, and safe routes do.

Minimal player-safe prompt:

> Trade is also listening. Gifts are also questions.

### Moon-naming note

If the Omen has **not** happened yet, player-facing material should use:

- `twin lights`
- `bright moon`
- `hungry moon`

Do **not** pre-label the moons as `Ashla` and `Bogan` in player-facing handouts before the Omen naming beat occurs in play.

If the Omen already occurred in Arc II, update the handout after play, not before.
