# 00 — Introduction & How to Use

**Dawn of the Je'daii** is a GM campaign guide for a Star Wars tabletop campaign set in **36,453 BBY (0 TYA)**—the year the Tho Yor gather Force-sensitive pilgrims and deliver them to Tython. It is written for **Foundry VTT** using **SW5e**, in the spirit of the *Living Force Campaign Guide*: a setting bible plus a playable adventure path.

<span data-gmb-src="00-introduction-how-to-use|h2|elevator-pitch" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## Elevator pitch

The player characters are not joining the Jedi Order. The Jedi do not exist yet.

They are among the first pilgrims called by a **Tho Yor**—an ancient pyramidal temple-ship. Session 1 begins **aboard that ship**, before anyone sets foot on Tython. Across the campaign they will explore the Tho Yor, survive Tython’s living Force storms, contact other pilgrim peoples, invent the philosophy of **Balance** between **Ashla** and **Bogan**, name the **Je'daii Order**, and seed the network of temples that will one day define the Order.

> **GM:** The party starts at **level 9**. They are already powerful individuals. Treat them as founders and first Masters-in-waiting, not padawans.

## Canon stance

- **Baseline:** Legends *Dawn of the Jedi* (comics) and related Tython / Je'daii lore.
- **Compression:** Canon spreads temple founding across centuries. This campaign **compresses** that into playable arcs plus explicit **time skips** so the table can found the Order and seed the nine temple sites without waiting millennia of table time.
- **Table fiction:** Where lore is silent, this guide invents playable detail. Appendix D marks Legends fact vs table fiction.

<span data-gmb-src="00-introduction-how-to-use|h2|spoiler-handout-policy" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## Spoiler & handout policy

- **GM-only:** Chapters 04 (secrets), 06–09 (faction/Order/Gate/calendar defaults), 13–23 (checklists, arcs, tools), Chapter 24 GM Master Timeline, Appendices A–B, D.
- **Safe for players:** Appendix C (Player Primer + Player Timeline); Chapter 10 Character Creation Requirements (planet list + Force paths + **Party Tho Yor calling** words); Chapter 24 Player Timeline Handout; Chapter 11 Session 0 boon *visions* only (not GM effects); Chapter 09 calendar **workshop** once landed (not the default Twin Measure until the table adopts or skips inventing). - Do **not** hand the full **Ninefold Code** at Session 0 (temple poetry spoils end-state). Calling words alone are safe; after Naming Council ratification, share the Creed freely.

# 01 — The Galaxy at 36,453 BBY

<span data-gmb-src="01-the-galaxy-at-36-453-bby|h2|the-date-that-matters" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## The date that matters

| Notation | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| **36,453 BBY** | Standard Legends dating (Before the Battle of Yavin) |
| **0 TYA** | **Tho Yor Arrival**—year zero for Tython’s later calendar |

Everything in this campaign happens around **0 TYA**: the Call, the gathering, the voyage, the landing, and the first years of Order-founding (compressed for play).

<span data-gmb-src="01-the-galaxy-at-36-453-bby|h2|what-does-not-exist-yet" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## What does not exist yet

Use this list to keep tone honest:

- No **Galactic Republic**, Senate, or Jedi Council
- No **Jedi Order** or **Sith Order** as later ages know them
- No standard **lightsabers** as common knightly weapons (metal blades, spears, Force-imbued tools; forge miracles come later)
- No familiar Outer Rim “cantina circuit” politics
- No reliable commercial **hyperlane** network the PCs can casually use—the **Tho Yor** is the miracle that crosses the Deep Core

<span data-gmb-src="01-the-galaxy-at-36-453-bby|h2|what-does-exist" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## What does exist

- Countless worlds with local cultures, wars, and Force traditions under many names
- Ancient mysteries (Gree, Kwa, Rakata-adjacent ruins and rumors—use as flavor, not mandatory truth)
- Force-sensitive individuals who hear something larger than themselves
- Eight gathering **Tho Yor** ships plus a ninth, greatest Tho Yor waiting at Tython

<span data-gmb-src="01-the-galaxy-at-36-453-bby|h2|the-call" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## The Call

Across the galaxy, Force-sensitives feel a summons—a song, a pressure behind the eyes, a dream of a black pyramid against a green world. Some find a Tho Yor buried in mountains or waiting in deserts. Others are guided by monks, shamans, or sheer obsession.

The **Order of Dai Bendu** on Ando Prime is the classic example: Talid monks who tended a buried Tho Yor for centuries until it woke. Other worlds have their own keepers and accidents. The PCs may come from any of these stories; unify them only once they share a deck.

<span data-gmb-src="01-the-galaxy-at-36-453-bby|h2|tone-of-the-age" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## Tone of the age

This is **mythic first contact**, not galactic intrigue:

- Wilderness survival on a Force-reactive world
- Philosophy under pressure (what is the Force *for*?)
- Multi-species founding politics
- Sacred technology that feels like divinity

When in doubt, ask: *Would this scene feel like the first chapter of a legend?*

<span data-gmb-src="01-the-galaxy-at-36-453-bby|h2|deep-core-isolation" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## Deep Core isolation

Tython sits in the **Deep Core**. Ordinary ships of this age do not casually come and go. Once the pilgrims land, they are effectively a closed ecosystem until the campaign invents reasons otherwise. That isolation is a feature: the table must build society, not phone home.

# 02 — The Tython System Catalog

<span data-gmb-src="02-the-tython-system-catalog|h2|tython-at-a-glance" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## Tython at a glance

**Tython** is a verdant, dangerous Deep Core world saturated in the Force. It is not merely a backdrop—it is an antagonist and teacher. When the pilgrims’ collective presence arrives, Tython answers with a **Force Storm** of legendary scale. Afterward, the planet continues to react to emotional and spiritual **imbalance**.

> **GM:** When the camp tips toward cruelty, fanaticism, despair, or absolutism, escalate weather, predators, quakes, or “the land rejecting you.” When Balance holds, ease the pressure—but never make Tython *safe*.

<span data-gmb-src="02-the-tython-system-catalog|h2|twin-moons-later-ashla-and-bogan" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## Twin moons (later Ashla and Bogan)

Tython has two companion moons—one bright, one dimmer/darker. At first landing they are simply **the twin lights**: visible teachers without settled names.

**GM Inspiration (mood only):** Before names, they are a clean coin and a bruised coin hung over a green world—useful for navigation arguments and omen weather, useless for theology until Speakers lock the words. After the Omen night, pointing at the sky becomes pointing at doctrine; every campfire debate gains a ceiling diagram. Keep early sessions visual and unnamed; let the renaming feel like inventing gravity.

| Later name | Association | Table use |
|------------|-------------|-----------|
| **Ashla** | Light, clarity, compassion, order | Omens of mercy, bright nights for rituals, soft glow in visions |
| **Bogan** | Dark, passion, destruction, power | Omens of hunger, red nights, trials of rage and ambition |

**When names arrive (locked):**

1. **Aboard the Tho Yor (Arc I):** pilgrims learn **Ashla** and **Bogan** as **Force concepts** (Meditation Core)—not as moon names.
2. **First dual-moon night after landing (Arc II end / Arc III omen):** Speakers adopt those words uniformly for the concepts; the camp then **names the moons after the concepts**.
3. **Arc IV Naming Council:** Code and Order bind Balance between Ashla and Bogan (moons + Force aspects already sharing vocabulary).

Until step 2, NPCs may say “bright moon / hungry moon,” “twin lights,” or homeworld nicknames.

<span data-gmb-src="02-the-tython-system-catalog|h2|continents-major-regions-overview" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## Continents & major regions (overview)

Detailed play sites live in **Chapter 05**. For catalog purposes:

- **Thyr** — Silent Desert, caverns that will host Qigong Kesh; stark forests and rocky plains
- **Masara** — Coastal plains and Edge Forest; future Bodhi and Padawan Kesh
- **Kato Zakar** — Violent continent; Ice Giant Range (future Stav Kesh)
- Ocean reaches — Deep Ocean sites (future Mahara Kesh)
- Mountain / rift / volcanic zones — Chasm (Anil Kesh), Tythos Ridge approaches (Kaleth / Vur Tepe), stone pinnacle under the greatest Tho Yor (Akar Kesh)

<span data-gmb-src="02-the-tython-system-catalog|h2|the-tho-yor-on-tython" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## The Tho Yor on Tython

Eight gathering ships deliver pilgrims and then **scatter across Tython**, resting (floating, buried, or embedded) at sites that will become temple-cities. A **ninth, largest Tho Yor** gathers the fleet and remains associated with the great pinnacle—later **Akar Kesh**, Temple of Balance.

<span data-gmb-src="02-the-tython-system-catalog|h2|other-worlds-of-the-tython-system-foreshadow-only" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## Other worlds of the Tython system (foreshadow only)

Canon later has non-Force-sensitives migrate to other planets in the system because Tython is unsafe for them. In this campaign:

- Mention sister worlds as distant lights or Tho Yor vision-glimpses
- Plant the seed that **not everyone who arrives can stay**
- Do not run interplanetary politics until/unless you extend past this guide’s scope

<span data-gmb-src="02-the-tython-system-catalog|h2|climate-travel" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## Climate & travel

Expect extremes: jungles, deserts that swallow sound, ice ranges, storms born of emotion, seas that heal or drown according to the Force’s mood. Travel between temple seeds is an adventure, not a montage—unless you deliberately call a **time skip** (Chapter 14).

# 03 — Peoples of the First Migration

More than a dozen Force-touched peoples board the Tho Yor. This chapter gives **culture cards** for the most useful table groups. Mix freely; PCs may be any SW5e species that fits your homebrew table’s tone.

<span data-gmb-src="03-peoples-of-the-first-migration|h2|common-friction-themes" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## Common friction themes

- Language barriers (gesture, Force empathy, and pidgin become campaign tools)
- “Whose Force is correct?”
- Warriors vs contemplatives vs scientists
- Those who want a fortress vs those who want a monastery
- Non-sensitives (or weakly sensitive kin) who followed a loved one and now regret it

<span data-gmb-src="03-peoples-of-the-first-migration|h2|culture-cards" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## Culture cards

<span data-gmb-src="03-peoples-of-the-first-migration|h3|dai-bendu-ando-prime-talid-monks" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
### Dai Bendu (Ando Prime — Talid monks)

- **Homeland:** Andobi Mountains; centuries tending a buried Tho Yor
- **Force fragment:** Monastic listening, prophecy, sacred geometry
- **Hooks:** Claim seniority (“we heard it first”); refuse violence; demand the Order keep a Dai Bendu name
- **Sample NPC:** Elder **Senn-Vora**—patient, immovable, secretly terrified the Call was a mistake

<span data-gmb-src="03-peoples-of-the-first-migration|h3|humans-many-worlds" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
### Humans (many worlds)

- **Homeland:** Varied; treat “Human” as diaspora, not one culture
- **Force fragment:** Folk magic, war-cults, scholar-orders, or raw talent with no doctrine
- **Hooks:** Ambition to lead multi-species camps; invent writing systems for a shared Code
- **Sample NPC:** **Kael Rind**—ex-mercenary who felt the Call mid-battle and deserted

<span data-gmb-src="03-peoples-of-the-first-migration|h3|wookiees" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
### Wookiees

- **Homeland:** Forest worlds (Kashyyyk traditions in deep past flavor)
- **Force fragment:** Life-web awareness, ancestral anger/protection
- **Hooks:** Defend groves; mistrust stone temples; life-debt politics
- **Sample NPC:** **Horruhn**—tree-singer who reads Tython’s forests like scripture

<span data-gmb-src="03-peoples-of-the-first-migration|h3|twi-leks" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
### Twi’leks

- **Homeland:** Hot-world clans and dancer-scholar lineages
- **Force fragment:** Emotion-reading, performance as prayer
- **Hooks:** Cultural diplomacy; seduction of power; clan rivalry imported to Tython
- **Sample NPC:** **Numa’Shar**—diplomat who brokers the first multi-camp truce

<span data-gmb-src="03-peoples-of-the-first-migration|h3|selkath" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
### Selkath

- **Homeland:** Oceanic worlds; water-law traditions
- **Force fragment:** Healing currents, legalistic ethics
- **Hooks:** Demand ocean access (Mahara Kesh seed); prosecute Force crimes early
- **Sample NPC:** Couple **Iil** and **Vorr**—healers who become living arguments for Mercy-as-Balance

<span data-gmb-src="03-peoples-of-the-first-migration|h3|cathar" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
### Cathar

- **Homeland:** Savanna pride cultures
- **Force fragment:** Predator focus, honor duels
- **Hooks:** Stav Kesh natural allies; challenge leaders who will not fight
- **Sample NPC:** **Sari Tor**—duelist who believes Balance is a blade held mid-swing

<span data-gmb-src="03-peoples-of-the-first-migration|h3|other-pilgrims-use-as-needed" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
### Other pilgrims (use as needed)

Sullustans, Noghri-adjacent stalkers, Devaronians, Ithorians, **Koorivar** (full writeup: **Chapter 12**), etc. Give each a one-line Force metaphor and one demand on the founding council.

<span data-gmb-src="03-peoples-of-the-first-migration|h2|party-composition-guidance-sw5e" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## Party composition guidance (SW5e)

- **Required:** homeland planet + Force manipulation — see **Chapter 10 Character Creation Requirements**
- Species may mix freely with planet (not pigeonholed)
- Avoid gear fantasies that require Republic-era megacorps
- Encourage mixed species—the Order’s founding identity is pluralism under Balance
- Pure tech-savants without Force use do **not** board; pair Engineer/Scout tech fantasy with a Force path (A–D in Ch 10)

# 04 — The Tho Yor

<span data-gmb-src="04-the-tho-yor|h2|what-they-are" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## What they are

The **Tho Yor** are enormous octahedron / pyramid temple-ships. **Eight** wake across the galaxy to gather Force-sensitives. They travel to Tython and gather about a **ninth, largest** Tho Yor. After arrival, the eight scatter across the planet; pilgrims later build temples at those resting places.

They feel less like starships and more like **living sanctuaries**: humming stone, light without torches, doors that open to those who *listen*.

**Ship clock:** Cabin lighting and sleep-pressure follow **Tython’s day/night**—**31.5 standard hours** per cycle—even before landing. Homeworld “days” already feel wrong; the ship is teaching them the green world’s rhythm (Chapter 09).

**Party ship:** The PCs wake aboard the gathering Tho Yor locked in **Session 0** (Chapter 10 calling). Murals, song, and pilgrim culture aboard may echo that calling (Science, Art, Healing, Knowledge, Teaching, Body, Mind, or Skill). Sister ships carry the other callings. The **ninth Tho Yor** (Balance / future **Akar Kesh**) is already at Tython—not the party ship.

<span data-gmb-src="04-the-tho-yor|h2|living-aboard-gm-inspiration-multi-session-home" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## Living aboard (GM Inspiration — multi-session home)

Treat the pyramid as a small city under vow, not a hallway between cutscenes. Days run **31.5 hours**; meal times argue; tempers fray on the “extra” hours. There is no market of coins—only craft, favor, status, and the quiet politics of who sleeps nearest the warm walls. The ship’s song is always underfoot: louder in Cores, thinner in Vaults, almost companionable in Dormitories until someone dreams the same dream as a stranger. Food is pilgrim stores plus what the ship provides in alcoves that appear when people stop hoarding. Violence earns null-corridors—sound dies, Force softens, offenders cool until they can share a deck again. From the Galleries, sister pyramids hang like other stories in progress; contact waits for Tython, but rivalry and kinship begin as silhouettes. Prep three Scene Cards per session (Chapter 21); let the party invent routines (watch, translation circle, craft corner) so the ship becomes *theirs* before the spines open.

<span data-gmb-src="04-the-tho-yor|h2|playable-zones-session-1" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## Playable zones (Session 1+)

Use these as map regions. Full **Great GM Scene Cards** (Inspiration + read-aloud) live in **Chapter 21**. Map 1 in **Chapter 23** covers the Threshold Crossroads. Boons hide in these zones (**Chapter 11**). The Kwa Gate sits off Machine-Spirit / Vaults (**Chapter 08**). Arc I Sessions 1–3 (Chapter 15) should bounce between these rooms—do not rush the landing.

<span data-gmb-src="04-the-tho-yor|h3|1-threshold-halls" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
### 1. Threshold Halls

Where pilgrims first awaken after boarding. Echoing corridors, soft gravity, murals of species that have not yet met.

**GM Inspiration:** See Chapter 21 — Threshold Halls (gold-standard sample). Canyon-wide sanctuary stone; late gravity; murals as patient surveillance; sealed handle-less door listening for a reply.

**Encounters:** Orientation; language barriers; a sealed door that opens only when three strangers meditate together; first boon niche.

**At the table:** Chapter 21 — Threshold Halls Scene Card.

<span data-gmb-src="04-the-tho-yor|h3|2-dormitories-of-the-called" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
### 2. Dormitories of the Called

Communal cells, personal relics left behind, dreams shared across walls.

**GM Inspiration:** Travel-pack smell; 31.5-hour sleep cycle; privacy weaves; shared dreams; theft and bonding; null-corridor discipline. Full prose: Chapter 21 — Dormitories Scene Card.

**Encounters:** Theft of a sacred object; nightmares that are *true*; forming the first party bond; *Milk of the Sword* bedroll niche (Ch 11).

**At the table:** Chapter 21 — Dormitories of the Called Scene Card.

<span data-gmb-src="04-the-tho-yor|h3|3-observation-galleries" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
### 3. Observation Galleries

Views of hyperspace-not-hyperspace: ribbons of Force light, starfields that look wrong, other Tho Yor as distant silhouettes. Tython thickens ahead with **two unnamed companion lights**—do not call them Ashla/Bogan yet.

**GM Inspiration:** Wound of glass; sister pyramids; steering rejected; green world thickening; twin lights unnamed. Full prose: Chapter 21.

**Encounters:** First sight of Tython; panic; someone tries to “steer” and is gently rejected by the ship; Quiet Paw Charm on a rail (Ch 11); optional mural of twin Force poles (support for Meditation Core concepts).

**At the table:** Chapter 21 — Observation Galleries Scene Card.

<span data-gmb-src="04-the-tho-yor|h3|4-meditation-cores" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
### 4. Meditation Cores

Spherical chambers where the ship’s song is loudest. Here the Tho Yor teaches the **concepts of Ashla and Bogan**—twin poles of the living Force (clarity/mercy/restraint vs passion/hunger/power). Balance omens begin here. These are **not** moon names yet.

**GM Inspiration:** Hollow sphere; song in the teeth; Ashla/Bogan as Force poles; mercy vs power trial; Bowl alcove. Full prose: Chapter 21.

**Encounters:** Shared dream-trial using the words Ashla vs Bogan; a PC must choose mercy or power; failure triggers ship-quake (foreshadow Tython); Bowl of First Rain alcove (Ch 11).

**At the table:** Chapter 21 — Meditation Core Scene Card.

<span data-gmb-src="04-the-tho-yor|h3|5-machine-spirit-interfaces" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>

\pagebreak

### 5. Machine-Spirit Interfaces

Glyph walls and crystal lattices. Science-minded PCs can *almost* understand.

**GM Inspiration:** Ticking lattices; understanding that bites; colder Kwa corridor with ring/twin-disk murals. Full prose: Chapter 21.

**Encounters:** Skill challenges to open a wing; risk of psychic backlash; rumor of builders (see Secrets); Ash-Glass Lens tray; path toward **Kwa Gate** (Ch 08).

**Kwa foreshadow murals (must-show in Arc I Session 2):** Wall reliefs in a colder corridor—ring-within-ring gate; two destination disks (bright / dark, rhyming with Ashla/Bogan concepts without naming moons); nine-fold refusal marks; figures of many species touching a circle together. Script feels **wrong for Tho Yor** (softer math, older curves). PCs may sketch or memorize; do **not** open the gate. Later Ch 08 reward: if they cite the mural, advantage on the first activation check **or** auto-refuse one illegal destination attempt.

**At the table:** Chapter 21 — Machine-Spirit Interface Scene Card.

<span data-gmb-src="04-the-tho-yor|h3|6-sealed-vaults" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
### 6. Sealed Vaults

Do not fully open in Arc I. Mark one vault with a nine-fold seal. Adjacent cold corridor leads to the Kwa Gate antechamber (glimpse only until activation rules are met)—same mural language as the Machine-Spirit foreshadow.

**GM Inspiration:** Cold wing; nine-fold seal; map dreams; guardian pressure; Gate ozone. Full prose: Chapter 21 — Sealed Vaults Scene Card.

**Encounters:** Partial glimpse—artifact map of Tython temple seeds; warning in an unknown tongue; a guardian echo; mural echo of the twin-disk gate.

**At the table:** Chapter 21 — Sealed Vaults Scene Card.

<span data-gmb-src="04-the-tho-yor|h3|7-confluence-amphitheater" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
### 7. Confluence Amphitheater

Natural meeting place for multi-species council. Thread of the Uncut Hour may be wound on a handrail (Ch 11).

**GM Inspiration:** Fair acoustics; terraces of peoples; translators sweating; civic birth or brawl. Full prose: Chapter 21.

**Encounters:** Must-play first council (Chapter 15); elect temporary speakers; avert a brawl that would imbalance the ship; meet Speakers (Chapter 22).

**At the table:** Chapter 21 — Confluence Amphitheater Scene Card.

<span data-gmb-src="04-the-tho-yor|h3|8-disembarkation-spines" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
### 8. Disembarkation Spines

Later: ramps/bridges of light when Tython receives them.

**GM Inspiration:** Ship becoming bridge; storm wind; home becoming silhouette; step-first tension. Full prose: Chapter 21.

**Encounters:** Arc II only—storm, scatter, or ordered landing.

**At the table:** Chapter 21 — Disembarkation Spines Scene Card.

<span data-gmb-src="04-the-tho-yor|h2|set-pieces" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## Set pieces

1. **The last Call** — Finish boarding; doors seal; no going home.
2. **Ship awakening** — Gravity shifts; song rises; strangers realize they are crew/cargo/pilgrims.
3. **Meeting of peoples** — First council.
4. **Balance omen** — Meditation Core trial.
5. **Approach** — Tython grows from a point of light to a living world; storm gathers.

<span data-gmb-src="04-the-tho-yor|h2|secrets-gm" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## Secrets (GM)

- **Builders:** Leave ambiguous. Offer conflicting hints—Gree arithmetic, Kwa Infinity Gate folklore, Rakatan hunger-tech as *warnings*, not answers. The **Kwa Gate** (Ch 08) is a concrete Kwa fingerprint without answering who built the Tho Yor.
- **The Vault Map:** Shows nine resting sites. Revealing it early gives players a campaign treasure map; delaying it preserves mystery.
- **The Ship Listens:** Extreme violence aboard may cause the Tho Yor to isolate offenders in a null-corridor until Balance returns.
- **Sister ships:** Other pilgrim groups arrive on other Tho Yor—each with a **Speaker** (Chapter 22). The PCs’ ship is not the only story—contact comes on Tython.
- **Boons:** Six ambiguous relics (Chapter 11) are meant to be found through exploration, not treasure chests labeled “+1 STR.”

> **GM:** Do not explain everything. Wonder is the Tho Yor’s primary treasure.

### THE NINEFOLD CODE OF THE JE’DAII

**I. Akar Kesh — The Temple of Balance**  
One current moves beneath the many names.  
The bright moon and the dark moon are seen upon one sea,  
yet the wise do not call shadow a lamp.  
Balance is not the equal enthroning of good and evil,  
but the ordering of every power toward what is good.  
When the center holds, storm becomes a path.

**II. Anil Kesh — The Temple of Science**  
Look into the deep places and name what is there.  
Measure the stone, the breath, the wound, the spark.  
What can be altered must first be understood.  
What is understood must still be judged by wisdom.  
Thus knowledge serves life,  
and does not devour it.

**III. Bodhi — The Temple of the Arts**  
What the hand makes, the soul declares.  
Song gathers the scattered before law can summon them.  
Image, feast, dance, and story teach kinship among strangers.  
Beauty is not the garment of truth,  
but its merciful face.  
Therefore create so that life may remember what is worthy.

**IV. Mahara Kesh — The Temple of Healing**  
Hold life as flame cupped against the wind.  
Heal where healing may be given;  
soothe where healing is denied.  
Do not mock death, and do not flee wisdom because of it.  
To honor the living, remember what returns beyond sight.  
Compassion is balance made visible.

**V. Kaleth — The Temple of Knowledge**  
Keep memory, lest each age proclaim itself the first.  
Seek the pattern hidden beneath event, vision, and star.  
Ignorance is sleep;  
knowledge is a burden accepted in humility.  
What ends in time is not lost to the greater current.  
Therefore let wisdom order what knowledge reveals.

**VI. Padawan Kesh — The Temple of First Discipline**  
The first temple is the body,  
and the first trial is the self.  
Let feeling arise,  
then set it in order before it becomes command.  
Let fear become watchfulness,  
desire become service,  
grief become tenderness,  
and anger become justice without cruelty.  
From the governed self comes a peace fit to guard another.  
Thus the learner begins in balance  
and walks toward it again.

**VII. Stav Kesh — The Temple of Martial Arts**  
All life meets contest beneath sun and storm.  
Train hand, eye, breath, and will,  
that strength may answer need.  
Yet the unmastered warrior is already conquered.  
The blade is a vow before it is a weapon.  
Strike only to protect, restrain, or restore what violence would consume.  
Only the balanced hand can contend  
without becoming what it opposes.

**VIII. Qigong Kesh — The Temple of Force Skills**  
Enter the silence until distance speaks as near.  
Hear the life beside you,  
the danger before you,  
and the path not yet trod.  
The Force moves through sense, thought, emptiness, and return.  
Guard the mind,  
and the far horizon will enter the heart without breaking it.  
Perception is the bridge between will and harmony.

**IX. Vur Tepe — The Forge**  
Bring fire to matter and purpose to fire.  
From passion rightly yoked comes strength;  
from strength, the power to shape.  
Let power build what life requires  
and break only what wisdom cannot spare.  
Victory without love forges chains;  
wise victory breaks them.  
The maker shall be judged  
by what remains when the heat is gone.

<span data-gmb-src="07-the-je-daii-order-founding|h2|the-great-journey-invented-late" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>

# 09 — Calendar of the First Year

Pilgrims arrive carrying **many homeworld calendars** and no shared Tython year. Treaties, harvests, temple journeys, and “how long until the Storm anniversary?” dissolve into argument without one. Inventing the calendar is the same class of founding myth as naming the Je'daii and Balance—**power + poetry**.

> **Legends anchor:** Year zero is **0 TYA (Tho Yor Arrival)** = **36,453 BBY**. Do not reinvent that.  
> **Tython physics (table fiction):** **1 Tython-day = 31.5 standard hours**; **1 Tython-year = 524 Tython-days**. Month names, festivals, and the Twin Measure below are **table fiction** (Appendix D)—this is **local Tython / early Je'daii time**, not later galactic standard.

The Tho Yor already runs the **31.5-hour** cabin cycle during the voyage (Chapter 04). Pilgrims feel jetlagged against every homeworld clock before they ever touch soil.

<span data-gmb-src="09-calendar-of-the-first-year|h2|why-calendars-matter" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## Why calendars matter

Without a shared measure:

- Speakers cannot schedule Confluence markets on the same “week”
- Time skips feel vague (“a while later”) instead of mythic (`Stonepath 4, 1 TYA`)
- Festival nights and Balance rites have nothing to hang on
- Later ages will invent galactic standard days/years; **your table is inventing Tython’s clock first**

The first calendar hung in the Confluence becomes scripture whether anyone meant it to.

<span data-gmb-src="09-calendar-of-the-first-year|h2|scene-the-counting-quarrel" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## Scene — The Counting Quarrel

**When:** Arc IV Naming Council (preferred), or late Arc III if the camp is already fighting over dates.

**Setup:** Speakers arrive with incompatible systems—Dai Bendu star-counts, Wookiee life-cycles, Selkath tide-law, Koorivar ledger-quarters, Cathar hunt-seasons. Someone pounds a stolen homeworld calendar stone on the table and declares it law. A scholar notes the ship’s long “nights” already matched Tython’s **31.5-hour** sun.

**PC job:** Broker **one Tython measure**. They need not invent every month in scene—open the workshop below, or declare the Twin Measure adopted “until wiser counting.”

**Rewards (pick one or more):**

- A PC names a month or festival
- A feast day tied to a PC’s homeland word
- A line in the Code about time / seasons / the moons
- Soft faction goodwill from the culture whose word was kept

<span data-gmb-src="09-calendar-of-the-first-year|h2|player-workshop-15-30-min" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## Player workshop (15–30 min)

Pick **3–5** prompts. Keep it loud and fun—naming nights, not spreadsheet night.

1. **Year zero** — Confirm **0 TYA** = Landing / Tho Yor Arrival (locked).
2. **What is a day / year?** — Lock physics: **day = 31.5 standard hours** (sunrise-to-sunrise); **year = 524 Tython-days** (Tython orbit). Not a later galactic year.
3. **Month names** — Each PC names one month after a homeworld word, a dead friend, a moon omen, or a temple seed.
4. **Sacred days** — Invent 3–5 festivals (Stormwake, Naming Night, Twinrise, First Blade, Quiet Peak vigil…).
5. **How imbalance marks time** — Optional flavor only: red Bogan nights / Ashla auroras shift festival omens (not alignment rails). If moons are not yet named when you draft the calendar, call halves “bright half / hungry half” until the Omen night (Ch 17).

> **Handout:** Write the calendar on a scrap of leather and hang it in the Confluence. If it burns in a Force Storm, the Order must remake it—together.

<span data-gmb-src="09-calendar-of-the-first-year|h2|default-calendar-the-twin-measure" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>

\pagebreak

## Default calendar — The Twin Measure

Use this **when players don’t care**. Share it after they skip inventing (or adopt it as interim law).

| Piece | Default |
|-------|---------|
| Era | **TYA** (Tho Yor Arrival); campaign present ≈ **0–1 TYA** |
| Day | **31.5 standard hours** (Tython sunrise-to-sunrise); same cycle the Tho Yor taught aboard |

| Dual rhythm | Each month: **Ashla half** days **1–20**, **Bogan half** days **21–40** (concept halves; moon names optional until Ch 17) |
| vs later galaxy | **Not** Republic/standard time. Local Tython measure only. |

## Tech & gear bans / rarities

<span data-gmb-src="10-sw5e-campaign-rules|h3|money-is-useless-episode-1" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
### Money is useless (Episode 1+)

**Republic credits, gem-coin, and homeworld currency buy nothing** aboard the Tho Yor or in the first Tython camps. There is no market that honors them.

If a PC wants gear, tools, comforts, or upgrades, they must:

1. **Craft it themselves** (tools, downtime, materials), or
2. **Commission an NPC crafter** (Chapter 22) by trading **materials, favors, protection, or status**—never credits.

Print this at Session 0. Enforce it the first time someone opens a purse.

**Generally unavailable / inappropriate**

- Mass-market blaster economies as in Clone Wars / OT eras
- Lightsabers as standard PC toys (special story rewards only, if at all)
- Holonet, Republic credits as universal currency, capital ships on demand

**Available**

- Cultural weapons and armor from homeworlds (what they carried onto the Call)
- Primitive-to-advanced local tech brought aboard
- Force techniques as primary “magic”
- Tho Yor amenities (food, water, shelter) until landing—then scarcity
- Crafted / favor-bartered gear only after Episode 1 begins

**Campaign rewards**

- Vur Tepe crafting breakthroughs
- Temple-blessed arms
- Rare Tho Yor crystals or tools (one or two per campaign)
- Crafter relationships (Torv, Shekk-Arra, Miri, Vesh & Loom—Ch 22)

## Force traditions vs Jedi/Sith labels

Do not use “Jedi” or “Sith” as mechanical faction names. Reflavor class features as:

- Clan war-arts, Dai Bendu rites, life-web song, ocean law, predator focus, etc.

Dark Side / imbalance mechanics (whatever your SW5e build uses) should hook to **Bogan excess** and Tython weather, not cartoon villain points alone.

## Boon card format

Name · Appearance · Vision · Find location · **GM effect** · Tell / cost

<span data-gmb-src="11-session-0-boons-of-the-tho-yor|h3|1-milk-of-the-sword" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
### 1. Milk of the Sword

- **Appearance:** A stoppered ceramic bottle of luminous blue milk that never spoils; the glaze shows a faint blade silhouette when turned.
- **Vision:** You kneel in a circle of broken spears. A child offers you a cup. When you drink, your arms become iron—and the child asks you to lift a gate no one else can move.
- **Find:** Dormitory niche beside a warrior’s abandoned bedroll.
- **GM effect:** While carrying the bottle (need not drink every fight), **+1 Strength**. First drink each dawn: temporary HP equal to proficiency bonus.
- **Tell:** After violence, the milk tastes metallic until you show restraint once.

<span data-gmb-src="11-session-0-boons-of-the-tho-yor|h3|2-thread-of-the-uncut-hour" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
### 2. Thread of the Uncut Hour

- **Appearance:** A spool of colorless thread that only shows silver under moonlight (or Ashla-light). The spool never empties—yet always feels nearly empty.
- **Vision:** You sew a wound in the sky. Each stitch buys a breath for someone dying. The last stitch is your own shadow, pulled tight.
- **Find:** Confluence Amphitheater seating, wound around a cracked handrail.
- **GM effect:** Once per long rest, as a reaction when an ally within 30 ft. drops to 0 HP, they drop to **1 HP** instead (thread flashes).
- **Tell:** After use, you hear a delayed echo of that ally’s scream for 1 hour.

<span data-gmb-src="11-session-0-boons-of-the-tho-yor|h3|3-coin-of-the-second-mouth" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
### 3. Coin of the Second Mouth

- **Appearance:** A heavy octagonal coin, blank on both faces until warmed in a palm—then one face shows a speaking mouth, the other a closed eye.
- **Vision:** You argue with yourself in a marketplace of species. Every lie costs a tooth. Every truth opens a door that was not there.
- **Find:** Threshold Halls mural base (falls into a PC’s hand when they touch a painted mouth).
- **GM effect:** **+1 Charisma**. Once per long rest, advantage on one Deception *or* Persuasion check—you choose when activating.
- **Tell:** If you choose Deception, a nearby stranger briefly sees your “second mouth.”

<span data-gmb-src="11-session-0-boons-of-the-tho-yor|h3|4-ash-glass-lens" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
### 4. Ash-Glass Lens

- **Appearance:** A palm-sized shard of volcanic glass; looking through it drains color from the world except Force-bright edges.
- **Vision:** You stand above a chasm of numbers. The lens shows which stones will lie and which will hold. A voice says *know first—bleed later.*
- **Find:** Machine-Spirit Interface crystal tray.
- **GM effect:** **+1 Intelligence**. Once per short rest, advantage on Investigation or a tech/Force-lore check to understand ancient devices (Tho Yor, Kwa Gate, Chasm).
- **Tell:** Overuse (3+/day) causes a one-round stun migraine (Con save DC 14).

<span data-gmb-src="11-session-0-boons-of-the-tho-yor|h3|5-quiet-paw-charm" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
### 5. Quiet Paw Charm

- **Appearance:** A carved predator tooth on a cord; soft fur wraps the root. It purrs—almost—when danger is near.
- **Vision:** You hunt barefoot through silent grass. Your heartbeat slows until the prey’s fear is louder than your steps. You choose not to kill—and the grass parts anyway.
- **Find:** Observation Gallery rail, tied as if left for the next scout.
- **GM effect:** **+1 Dexterity**. Advantage on Stealth while not wearing heavy armor.
- **Tell:** When you choose loud violence, the charm goes cold until you succeed on a Stealth check again.

<span data-gmb-src="11-session-0-boons-of-the-tho-yor|h3|6-bowl-of-first-rain" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
### 6. Bowl of First Rain

- **Appearance:** A shallow stone bowl that fills with clear water each dawn; steam smells like homeworld rain.
- **Vision:** You wash blood from strangers’ hands. The water darkens, then clears. A dying world drinks, and green returns for one season.
- **Find:** Meditation Core alcove, offered by a soft light when a PC shows mercy unprompted—or placed for the most compassionate PC after Session 0 vision.
- **GM effect:** **+1 Wisdom**. Bowl water: once per dawn, cast a free 2nd-level *cure*-equivalent (or SW5e healing power of similar tier) without spending the resource—touch range, one creature.
- **Tell:** If used to heal someone who then immediately commits cruelty, the bowl stays dry for 24 hours.

- Session 0: each player receives vision + name/look only.
- Sessions 1–2: guide exploration so each PC can **find** their assigned boon (or discover leftovers).

<span data-gmb-src="12-species-spotlight-koorivar|h3|biology-and-appearance" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
### Biology and Appearance
Koorivar are tall near-humanoids, typically around 1.9 meters without counting the horn, with reptilian traits. Their scaly skin appears in many shades—black, blue, green, magenta, mauve, red, or tan. Banded ridges mark the brow and nose. The cranium bears small twisting horns around a central spiral horn that develops in adolescence and reaches full stature in adulthood. That spiral is a living badge of status: larger, well-kept horns signal wealth and dominance. Loss or severe damage to the primary horn is a social catastrophe among their kind. Their speech is soft and gesture-rich—whispering hisses paired with precise body language—making them acute readers of mood and unspoken intent.

<span data-gmb-src="12-species-spotlight-koorivar|h3|society-and-culture" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
### Society and Culture
Business craft is culture. Children are raised to bargain, keep ledgers, and read a room; marriages and alliances often look like mergers. Corporate pragmatism dominates: ambition and calculated self-interest are virtues, though Force-touched pilgrims and other exceptions break that mold. In deep antiquity the species still dwells on its **lost homeworld**; only in later eras do they lease and then purchase the Inner Rim world they name **Kooriva**, spreading as merchants and nobles across the galaxy. Schools teach negotiation as an art. Despite their reputation for hard dealing, Koorivar are social beings who thrive in markets, councils, and crowded trade floors.

**Female Names.** Denaria Kee, Leosa Vantare, Denlace Sachoore

### Koorivar Traits
As a Koorivar, you have the following special traits.

***Ability Score Increase.*** Your Charisma score increases by 2, and your Intelligence score increases by 1.

***Age.*** Koorivar mature slightly slower than Humans. The spiral horn develops in adolescence and reaches full stature in adulthood. They commonly live a bit longer than a Human century.

***Alignment.*** Corporate pragmatism dominates Koorivar culture. Individuals lean toward ambition and calculated self-interest, but Force-touched pilgrims can break that mold.

***Size.*** Koorivar are tall humanoids, typically around 1.9 meters without counting the horn. Your size is Medium.

***Speed.*** Your base walking speed is 30 feet.

***Coercive.*** You have proficiency in your choice of Persuasion or Deception.

***Gestural Fluency.*** Koorivar speech leans on whisper and body language. You have advantage on Wisdom (Insight) checks made to read a creature’s body language, mood, or unspoken intent. This trait has no effect on constructs or droids that lack readable body language.

***Iron Will.*** Centuries of haggling and hard bargaining have hardened your focus. When you make a Wisdom saving throw, you can use your reaction to gain advantage on the roll. Once you use this trait, you can’t use it again until you finish a short or long rest.

***Dealmaker.*** Whenever you make a Charisma (Persuasion) check related to buying, selling, contracts, or haggling, you are considered proficient in Persuasion and add double your proficiency bonus to the check, instead of your normal proficiency bonus (if any).

***Climate Adaptation.*** You have advantage on Constitution saving throws made to avoid exhaustion due to extreme heat.

***Languages.*** You can speak, read, and write Galactic Basic, Koorivar, and one additional language of your choice.

**When:** First night after landing when both companion moons are clearly visible (end of Arc II or opening of Arc III).

# 21 — Great GM Scene Craft

This campaign asks you to *set the stage and react*—the *How to be a Great GM* approach (Guy Sclanders): cinematic openings, situations over scripts, NPCs with wants, descriptions that invite action.

Your stated struggle is **descriptions**. Prep two layers:

- **GM Inspiration** — private prose you read before the session to *see* the place or person (Traviss grit, Allston wry competence, Zahn precise mystery). Longer is fine. Players never hear this verbatim.
- **Read-aloud** — 4–6 short sentences you *speak*. End on the urgent hook. Never a novel at the table.

<span data-gmb-src="21-great-gm-scene-craft|h2|the-scene-card-copy-this-every-time" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## The Scene Card (copy this every time)

```
TITLE: ____________________   TONE: (wonder / dread / tense / sacred)
GM INSPIRATION (private; ~½ page for Tho Yor rooms; shorter elsewhere):
  [see → hear → Force → smell/touch; end on playable tension]
READ-ALOUD (4–6 sentences; END on the urgent hook):
1.
2.
3.
4.
SENSES: See | Hear | Smell | Touch | Force
EXITS / INTERACTABLES: (what can they do right now?)
NPC WANT IN FRAME: (one line)
SECRETS / CLUES: (portable—drop anywhere)
```

**Inspiration vs read-aloud**

- Inspiration is for *you*—mood, blocking, what the room wants.
- Read-aloud is what you *speak*—then stop talking and let them act.
- Never script how they solve it.

<span data-gmb-src="21-great-gm-scene-craft|h2|style-bible-gm-inspiration" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## Style bible (GM Inspiration)

Write like a good Star Wars novel page, not a spell description:

1. **Sensory order:** what they see → hear → feel in the Force → smell/touch.
2. **Bodies and duty** (Traviss): sweat, weight of gear, who owes whom.
3. **Competence under pressure** (Allston): someone already trying a smart/stupid fix.
4. **Quiet precision** (Zahn): one wrong detail that hints at a larger machine.
5. **End on tension:** a door, a choice, a want—something the table can grab.
6. **Era hygiene:** no Jedi/Sith order jargon; no lightsabers as toys; early Arc I ship prose does **not** name the companion moons Ashla/Bogan (concepts may appear in the Core only).
7. **No fog words:** cut “inexplicable,” “mysterious energy,” “you feel a presence” without a concrete image.

**Rules of the read-aloud**

- Five short sentences beat one long speech.
- Vague emotional words first (“ancient,” “hungry,” “too quiet”), details on demand.
- End on the **most urgent** thing so players stop listening and start deciding.
- Never script how they solve it.

<span data-gmb-src="21-great-gm-scene-craft|h2|session-prep-sheet-one-page" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## Session prep sheet (one page)

Before each session, fill only:

1. **Strong start** (first 5 minutes already in motion)
2. **3 locations** (Scene Cards—skim Inspiration, speak Read-aloud)
3. **3 NPCs** (want / fear / lever + Inspiration) — see Chapter 22
4. **8 secrets & clues** (unordered list; plant wherever play goes)
5. **1 reward / boon beat** (Chapter 11)
6. **Storm Clock** note (Chapter 10)

If you freeze at the table: ask what the nearest NPC wants next.

### Scene Card — Threshold Halls (first boarding)

- **Tone:** wonder + disorientation
- **GM Inspiration:** The Threshold is not a corridor so much as a canyon carved by someone who measured in prayers. The stone is matte black shot with gold veins that do not quite follow stress lines—more like scar tissue that learned geometry. Light wells up from inside the walls; there are no sconces to steal, no seams for a knife. Gravity here is polite and slightly late: boots land a half-breath after the mind expects them to, which makes first-timers look drunk and veterans look careful. Murals run the length of the hall—faces of species the party has never met, painted with a patience that feels like surveillance. The ship’s song is a low harmonic under the sternum, the kind of note that makes teeth itch and arguments quiet. Pilgrims cluster by language the way soldiers cluster by unit; someone is already trying to invent hand-signs; someone else is crying without sound because the Call-door behind them will not reopen. Ahead, three mouths of passage yawn open (dormitories, galleries, deeper ship). One door stands shut without a handle, as if the builders disliked the idea of knobs. The Force in this hall feels like a held breath—not hostile, not kind. It is waiting to see whether these strangers will become a people or a riot.
- **Read-aloud:** The corridor is wider than a canyon and quieter than a tomb. Soft light bleeds from the stone itself—no torches, no seams. Murals of faces you have never met watch you with patient eyes. Your footsteps land late, as if gravity is thinking. Ahead, three passages yawn open… and one door stands shut without a handle. Something behind it is listening for a reply.
- **Senses:** matte black-gold stone; low harmonic hum; cold air tasting of rain on metal; Force like a held breath
- **Exits:** left dormitory wing; right galleries; forward sealed door; back (sealed Call-door)
- **NPC want:** a frightened pilgrim wants someone else to go first
- **Secrets:** door opens if three strangers meditate together; a boon niche glows faintly (Ch 11)

### Scene Card — Dormitories of the Called

- **Tone:** intimate / unsettled home
- **GM Inspiration:** The dormitory wing smells like travel packs opened too fast—old leather, dried fruit, sweat, and the sharp green of someone’s homeworld herb stuffed into a pouch for luck. Cells open off a spine corridor: some are true rooms with sleeping niches cut into the stone; some are little more than alcoves with a lip of railing and a privacy weave hung by pilgrims who refuse to be naked before strangers. Soft light dims and brightens on a **31.5-hour** cycle that already argues with every body’s clock. People dream loudly here. Sometimes the dreams agree—same black pyramid, same green world—and strangers wake staring at each other like co-conspirators. Personal relics sit on shelves the ship somehow provides: prayer beads, clan knives, a child’s carved animal, a ledger with no currency left that matters. Theft happens because fear happens. Bonding happens because the alternative is sleeping alone next to a wall that hums like a throat. If violence spikes, a side passage can go *null*—sound dies, Force softens, and the ship isolates the problem until someone remembers they are not predators in a cage but pilgrims in a sanctuary. A faint niche may glow near a bedroll (*Milk of the Sword*, Ch 11). The playable tension: whose rules govern the night—homeworld custom, or the ship’s quiet insistence that strangers share breath?
- **Read-aloud:** Soft light breathes on a longer day than your bones expect. Sleeping niches line the walls; packs and relics claim shelves that should not exist. Someone dreams in a language you almost understand. A privacy weave flutters. From somewhere deeper, the ship’s song presses against your ribs—and a niche by an empty bedroll glows like a held secret.
- **Senses:** dim cycle-light; snores and whispered translations; herb/sweat/stone; Force like shared dreaming
- **Exits:** back to Threshold; side null-corridor (if Storm Clock high); deeper toward galleries/cores
- **NPC want:** a pilgrim wants their stolen relic returned without starting a feud
- **Secrets:** true nightmares; first party bond; boon bedroll niche (Ch 11)

### Scene Card — Observation Galleries

- **Tone:** cosmic awe
- **GM Inspiration:** The galleries are a wound of transparent stone looking into travel that refuses to be hyperspace. Outside, ribbons of Force-light braid around distant pyramid silhouettes—other Tho Yor, other cargoes of the Called—close enough to feel like neighbors and far enough to feel like myths. Stars smear in directions that make navigators swear under their breath and then go quiet, because swearing does not help. Ahead, a green point thickens into a world; two companion lights attend it—one clean, one bruised—still without names. Do not let yourself or the players christen them yet. The rails are warm where many hands have gripped them. A glyph cluster near a “helm” alcove rejects steering attempts with the polite firmness of a parent taking a knife from a child: the panel cools, the song dips, and the ship continues. Someone will try to count the pyramids. Someone will bargain with gods that are not listening. Someone will laugh because if they do not laugh they will scream. Optional twin-pole mural on an inner wall supports later Core teaching without moon labels. The tension: panic wants a pilot; the ship wants witnesses.
- **Read-aloud:** The gallery is a wound of glass looking into something that is not hyperspace. Ribbons of Force-light braid around distant pyramid silhouettes—other Tho Yor, other pilgrims. Stars smear wrong. Far ahead, a green point thickens into a world with two companion lights—one clean, one bruised—still without names. Someone gasps. ### Scene Card — Meditation Core

- **Tone:** sacred trial
- **GM Inspiration:** The Core is a hollow sphere large enough to make voices small. The ship’s song is loudest here—felt in teeth, in the hollow behind the eyes, in the places people keep their private vows. Pilgrims enter barefoot if they are wise; boots feel like insults. The air tastes faintly of rain and copper. When the teaching comes, it does not arrive as scripture on a wall. Two words settle into the mind as if they had always been waiting under the tongue: **Ashla** and **Bogan**—clarity that costs, hunger that costs—Force poles, not moon names. The chamber offers trials the way a blade offers an edge: mercy that spends strength, power that spends kindness. Refuse both and the floor may choose for you—a tremor that foreshadows Tython’s intolerance for imbalance. An alcove holds a shallow bowl that smells of first rain (*Bowl of First Rain*, Ch 11). Watch faces: zealots brighten; soldiers brace; children ask if the words are names of people. Your job is not to lecture theology. Your job is to let the ship teach, then ask what the PCs do with the scar.
- **Read-aloud:** The chamber is a hollow sphere. The ship’s song is loud enough to feel in your teeth. Two words arrive that no homeworld taught you—*Ashla. Bogan.*—clarity that costs, and hunger that costs. The Core offers a choice that is not a choice: mercy that costs strength, or power that costs kindness. Choose, or the floor will choose for you.
- **Secrets:** **Concept discovery** of Ashla/Bogan as Force poles (not moon names); Balance omen; ship-quake on refusal/extremes; Bowl alcove (Ch 11)

### Scene Card — Machine-Spirit Interface

- **Tone:** puzzle / danger
- **GM Inspiration:** Crystal lattices tick like insect thoughts along walls that want to be understood by the wrong kind of mind. Glyphs rearrange when you look away—Zahn’s kind of wrongness, precise and unfriendly to ego. Science-minded pilgrims lean in like hunters; mystics flinch because the Force here is thin and sharp, like a tool left on a workbench. ### Scene Card — Confluence Amphitheater

- **Tone:** civic birth / fragile peace
- **GM Inspiration:** The Amphitheater is the ship’s answer to a city square: terraces of stone descending toward a flat speaking floor, acoustics so fair that a soft voice carries and a shout embarrasses itself. ### Scene Card — Force Storm (arrival)

- **Tone:** mythic catastrophe
- **GM Inspiration:** Arrival is not weather; it is the Force having an opinion. Lightning forks with intention—toward fear, toward cruelty, toward the loudest imbalance. Wind carries half-heard names, some of them the PCs’. The ninth Tho Yor hangs above a stone pinnacle like a judge who does not need a gavel. Sister pyramids stutter in and out of cloud. Pilgrims scream in a dozen languages that somehow mean the same prayer: *hold*. Calm them and the Storm Clock eases; rule them by terror and it advances. Bodies will be wet, burned, euphoric, catatonic. This is the last moment the ship is uncomplicatedly *home*.
- **Read-aloud:** Lightning thinks. Wind carries names you have not spoken aloud. The ninth Tho Yor hangs above a stone pinnacle like a verdict. Your ship shivers. Pilgrims scream in a dozen languages that somehow mean the same prayer: *hold*.

### 1. Senn-Vora — *Tho Yor of the Quiet Peak* (party ship default)

- **GM Inspiration:** Senn-Vora moves like someone who has spent centuries listening to stone that might never wake—and then it did. When pilgrims brawl, he does not shout first—he arrives in the silence after the first punch and makes that silence useful. He wants an Order that listens. He fears the Call was vanity dressed as destiny. Use him early aboard: translation disputes, naming arguments, the soft veto of a man who will teach the word that becomes *Je'daii* if the table earns it.
- **Species:** Talid (Dai Bendu)

- **Want / fear / lever:** Want Order-as-listening · Fear Call was vanity · Lever: legitimacy of names

## Opening crawl (player-safe)

**DAWN OF THE JE’DAII**

There was a time before the Jedi.
Before the Sith.
Before the Force was divided, named, and turned into a weapon.

The galaxy was older then, and far less certain of itself. Kingdoms rose beneath unfamiliar suns. Clans fought over worlds that had no place on any chart. Across a thousand cultures, the Force was worshiped as a god, feared as a curse, studied as a mystery, or dismissed as the fancy of mystics.

Then came the Call.

It was not a voice, though some heard one.

It was not a dream, though many woke with the same image burned into their memory: a black pyramid beneath an alien sky, waiting on a green and distant world.

Some followed willingly.
Some left home searching for answers.
Some resisted until the Call became stronger than fear.

Across scattered worlds, ancient vessels stirred.

Some descended from the heavens without flame, engine, or signal. Others rose from beneath stone and sand, shedding the weight of ages as though waking from a long sleep.

No pilot emerged.
No messenger offered an explanation.

Yet each vessel had come for the same purpose.

It had come for those who could feel the Force.

You entered by choice, by necessity, or because some part of you already understood that refusal was no longer possible.

Within the vessel, you found strangers. Warriors and healers. Scholars and hunters. Mystics, exiles, and wanderers drawn from peoples whose names had never crossed the stars between you.

The doors closed.

The vessel rose.

Your worlds vanished into the darkness.

Now the black pyramid carries you beyond every border and every chart, toward a planet none of you has seen and a destiny none of you chose together.

You do not know who built the vessel.

You do not know why it chose you.

You do not know the name of the world waiting ahead.

You know only this:

When the journey ends, the galaxy will never be the same.

## You are pilgrims of the Tho Yor

The year is **36,453 before the Battle of Yavin**—an age so old that the Republic and the Jedi Order do not exist. You felt a Call in the Force and boarded an ancient pyramidal ship: a **Tho Yor**. Session one begins aboard that ship. You have not yet walked the world called **Tython**.

<span data-gmb-src="appendix-c-handouts-player-primer|h2|what-you-know" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## What you know

- You are Force-touched (or bound to someone who is)
- Others from many species share the decks
- The ship is taking you somewhere that *matters*
- Home is behind sealed doors

<span data-gmb-src="appendix-c-handouts-player-primer|h2|what-you-do-not-know-yet" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## What you do not know (yet)

- Why you were chosen
- Who built the Tho Yor
- What Tython will demand of you

<span data-gmb-src="appendix-c-handouts-player-primer|h2|tone" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## Tone

This is a founding legend: survival, wonder, and the birth of a philosophy. You will help create the **Je'daii**—students of Balance—not join a finished order of Jedi.

<span data-gmb-src="appendix-c-handouts-player-primer|h2|character-tips" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## Character tips

- Level 9 SW5e heroes: capable, dangerous, unfinished as a culture
- Lean into cultural tradition over later-era Jedi jargon
- Bring a reason you answered the Call
- **Homeland:** pick one planet from Chapter 10 / 24 (species free)
- **Force:** you must manipulate the Force somehow (Forcecasting class, Force archetype, Force-Sensitive feat, or innate Force species such as Miraluka)—see Chapter 10 lists
- **As a table, choose your ship’s calling**—one word you all share: Science, Art, Healing, Knowledge, Teaching, Body, Mind, or Skill (not Balance). Your GM will tell you what that means for the voyage.
- Later, when the Order is named, you will help write **one verse** of the Je'daii Creed for that calling. Your GM keeps the full Creed until then.

<span data-gmb-src="appendix-c-handouts-player-primer|h2|player-timeline-through-pickup" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## Player Timeline (through Pickup)

See **Chapter 24 — Player Timeline Handout** for the shareable Pre-Republic / homeland timeline ending at the Tho Yor Pickup. Do not share the GM Master Timeline section of Chapter 24.

# Appendix D — Lore Sources & Canon Notes

<span data-gmb-src="appendix-d-lore-sources-canon-notes|h2|legends-fact-baseline" aria-hidden="true" style="display:contents"></span>
## Legends fact (baseline)

- 36,453 BBY / Tho Yor Arrival gathers Force-sensitives to Tython
- **0 TYA** as year zero of the Tho Yor Arrival calendar
- Eight Tho Yor + ninth greatest at Tython; Force Storm on arrival
- Pilgrims form the **Je'daii Order**; Balance of Ashla & Bogan named for Tython’s moons
- Nine temples built at Tho Yor resting sites over a long early history
- Much later: Despot War, Force Wars, and eventual Jedi Order—**out of scope** here

- Exact Session 1 ship layout and named zones
- Compressed temple founding into one campaign with time skips
- Named Speakers, crafters, walk-ons, and rivals (Chapter 22)
- Storm Clock mechanic
- Specific council agendas and Code workshop procedure
- Session 0 boon pool and effects (Chapter 11)
- Kwa Gate limited to Ashla/Bogan (Chapter 08)
- Great GM Scene Cards and four Foundry map briefs (Chapters 21 and 23)
- Local pre-Call centuries on listed homeworlds in Chapter 24 (tagged [T])
- Chapter 10 Character Creation: locked homeland planets, free species, mandatory Force paths (SW5e class/archetype/species/feat lists)
- Chapter 12 Koorivar species (worksheet-balanced SW5e traits; Legends lore)
- Chapter 09 Twin Measure: **31.5-hour** Tython-day, **524-day** year, 13×40 months + 4 Festival Nights, calendar workshop (TYA year-zero remains Legends; local time ≠ later galactic standard)
- Arc I Tho Yor teachings: Ashla/Bogan as **Force concepts**; Kwa Gate foreshadow murals; moons named after concepts on first dual-moon night (Ch 02 / 17)
- Chapter 13 Session 0 / Episode 1 checklists; moneyless craft-or-favor economy (Ch 10)
- Session 0 **Party Tho Yor calling** → locks gathering ship + destined Kesh (eight options; Balance / Akar Kesh reserved for the ninth Tho Yor already on Tython) (Ch 10 / 05 / 13)
- **Ninefold Code of the Je'daii** (Ch 07)—full Creed; party rewrites one fold at Arc IV (Ch 18); Force Wars / Jedi–Sith twisted inheritances are out-of-scope flavor only

- Whether Forcesaber-like tech appears (default: no)
